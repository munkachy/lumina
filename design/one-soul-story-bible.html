One Soul

You are an angel, and one life is yours to keep.

Target 23,700 words Choices 142 Scenes 12 Ward Ruth Kowalczyk, 1953–2023

This is the whole plan for the first game — the shape, the mechanics, the twelve scenes and every beat inside them. The prose is bracketed; nothing here is finished writing. It exists so the structure can be argued with before 24,000 words get built on top of it.

Two rules fall out of those numbers and both are load-bearing.

Never more than about 170 words without a decision. That is the pace of the whole form. A page you only read is a page the player is losing interest on.

The chapters never branch. Scene 1 always leads to scene 2. What your choices decide is not where you go but whether you succeed when you get there — the stats carry the memory forward. Branching the story itself is arithmetic suicide: a twenty-page story with two-way branches needs over a million pages written.

02The stat sheet

Three opposed pairs, one free-standing virtue, and a second column that does not belong to you.

ValorPatience

Strike now, or keep the long watch. One slider, two names — high Valor is low Patience.

SeverityMercy

How you meet her failure. Both are real virtues. Neither is the answer.

SplendorHiddenness

Manifest — light, dread, do not be afraid — or work entirely through ordinary means: a delayed bus, a friend who calls.

Charitynot opposed

The only stat with nothing on the other end, because it isn’t a trade-off. It is what all the rest is for.

03Combat: three verbs

Daniel 10 settles what angelic conflict is. The messenger was not wounded and was not beaten — he was withstood for twenty-one days, and Michael had to come and relieve him. The loser is delayed, and delay is lethal, because your ward is deciding right now.

STAND

Put yourself on a thing — a person, a place, an hour, a suggestion being offered. Nothing else can act on it while you are there.

Cost: you can stand on one thing only. An angel is where it acts.

PRESS

Move against someone who is standing. You cannot damage him. You cost him time.

Cost: exactly the same time, out of your own clock.

SHOW

Hand something over out of your own mind. Breaks a lock instantly — you show him a thing he cannot bear to look at.

Cost: whatever you show is true, and now permanently his.

No health bar. The resource is the clock, and the clock is Ruth walking toward the moment. It ticks on screen. Every action spends a segment. You lose by arriving late.

Two angels contending over the same object are locked: neither can move, neither can be hurt, and nothing resolves it except a third party arriving or the decision being made somewhere else without either of them. That is Jacob at the ford. That is twenty-one days.

05Four threads

Scrupulosity — 3.3 → 3.6 → 8 → 9 → 11 What you asked to know — 1.13 → all of 3 → 11 Rudy — 2.4 → 3.4 → 6 → 9 → 11 Halina’s prayers — 2.5 → 3.2 → 3.8 → 12

The spine is scrupulosity, and it is what makes this a Catholic game rather than a game with Catholics in it. A genuinely holy, genuinely uneducated grandmother forms a nine-year-old well. Then the girl’s own conscience turns on her, quietly, in 1962, and nobody in her world has the vocabulary for it. She spends fifty years experiencing God as a source of terror rather than mercy, and drifts — not out of unbelief, out of exhaustion.

It also gives Rudy a strategy that is worth writing. He never tempts her to anything gross. He tempts her to more religion, badly done. He is the voice that says say it again, you got a word wrong. And it is his mistake, because a scrupulous soul is a soul that will not stop looking at God, and he has spent seventy years keeping her eyes on the one thing he cannot stand. That lands in scene 11.

SCENE ONE

The Assembly

Before there was anything to date it by
Words 1,900Choices 22Images 2

Character creation disguised as the only decision that ever mattered. Nobody picks a stat; everybody makes a choice and finds out afterwards what they are.

1.1You are

Creation, whole and instant. Three things known at once: what you are, what you are for, that you did not make yourself. The retrospective frame is stated flatly here — you are remembering this from the far end of a human life, which is why the story will keep reaching for human comparisons you do not have yet. Pittsburgh and Dobson Street named on page one.

No choiceRoughly 300 words before the first decision. The only place in the game that runs long without one.
1.2The throne

One image, held: a throne, fire on it that burns nothing, a floor like ice with light coming up through it. Enough light to know exactly who that is and what He is owed — and not enough to make refusing impossible. The gap is deliberate and you understand that immediately.

1.3The court

Thousands, in ranks, no two the same kind of creature. Sparrows come in flocks; nothing here does. God made one of you and stopped. Then the speech rule, in plain words: hand a thing over and the other knows it entire; nothing can be garbled and nothing can be faked. There are only two things you can ever do with what you know — keep it, or give it. And everyone can tell which you did.

  • One orienting choice with almost no weight, to teach the interface before it matters.
1.4One of them shuts

The first sin in the history of creation announces itself as a silence. Somewhere ahead of you a mind turns its back and stops letting anyone in, and every mind in the room feels the door close at once. Nothing like this has happened. There has not been enough time for anything to have happened.

  • Go toward it, now, before it spreads. valor %+20
  • Hold. You will not move until you know what you are moving against. valor %−20 · severity %+10
  • Speak. Ask the court, out loud, what has happened. splendor %+20
  • Turn back toward the throne and stay there. charity %+15 · splendor %−10
1.5The visionIMAGE 1

Full bleed, no caption, no text on the page. A room, early, the light still grey. A girl on the edge of a bed with the covers shoved around, barefoot, hair not done. Standing in the corner is a light — not a lamp, upright, about as tall as a person. She is looking at it and is not afraid and her mouth is moving.

Nobody explains it. Nobody in the room knows. That is the test: you are shown one thing and asked to say yes to it without being told what it means.

Art referenceHenry Ossawa Tanner, The Annunciation, 1898, Philadelphia Museum of Art — a barefoot peasant girl on a rumpled bed, no halo, and Gabriel rendered as a column of gold light. Already on-model for the no-faces rule.
1.6His reading of it

He turns around and hands it to all of you at once: that is not one of us; that is dirt with breath blown into it; it sleeps, it gets sick, it dies — and He is going down into that, all the way down, and when He does we will be asked to kneel. Every word is true. He cannot lie. What he leaves out is the only thing that matters — and that is good — and he can leave it out because it is not a fact but a judgement, and judgement is the one thing that stays inside where nobody can reach it.

  • “Be silent.” severity %+25 · valor %+10
  • “Show me, then. I will look, and I will answer you.” mercy %+20 · sets heard_him_first
  • Say nothing to him. Say it to the ones nearest you: don’t go over there. charity %+20 · splendor %−15
  • Say nothing to anyone. Look at the throne. patience %+25 · charity %+10
Delayed branchListening is not sin and costs nothing morally. What it costs is that the first interpretation you ever heard of that image was his. Surfaces once, in 11.2, when the game asks whether you are reading Ruth or reading her through him.
1.7Who is like God?

Four words, from the middle of the room, from nobody important. It answers nothing he said. It walks out of the argument and stands somewhere else, and that turns out to be the only move there is — because the question was never whether she is better than you, it is whether He is God.

  • Take up the cry so the whole court hears it from you. splendor %+25 · valor %+15
  • Take up the cry — and go stand with the ones still wavering. splendor %−20 · charity %+25 · mercy %+15
  • Go to him, whoever he is, and stand at his shoulder. valor %+25 · splendor %+10
  • Answer once, quietly, then look at nothing but the throne. patience %+25 · splendor %−25 · charity %+15
1.8The expulsionTUTORIAL

They do not simply drop. They hold position, and the ones who have not chosen yet are behind them, and the line of light has to take the floor back a rank at a time. Ninety seconds, three choices, and you use all three verbs: you STAND on a mind that has not decided, something PRESSES you and you are locked with something older and brighter that cannot hurt you and cannot be moved, and the only way out is to SHOW it something.

Why it is hereTwelve thousand years later, in a parking lot in Sharpsburg, the player uses the identical three verbs on the identical problem — and recognises it. That recognition is the entire reason the fight is in scene 1.
1.9A third of the room

Gone. Not thrown, exactly — they turned away from the only thing holding them up and kept turning, and there was nowhere else to go. Where they were is not dark. It is worse than dark: a shape in the middle of the light that the light does not enter.

1.10The orders

Nine of them, explained plainly and quickly for a player who has never heard any of this. Those who stand nearest the fire and do nothing but burn. Those given a nation. Those given a Church. Those who go where the fallen are and hold them off and consider that enough. And at the bottom — the word is not an insult, it is accurate — the last of the nine. Not to a nation, not to a Church, not to an army. To one person. One. Each.

ToneYou do not feel cheated, and the narration says so, because later you will meet minds that assume you must. You have just watched a third of everything throw itself away over whether being given a thing is worse than taking it. You are not making that mistake in the first hour.
1.11The name

“You have a name. You won’t use it here and neither will I — it isn’t a word, it is the thing you are, and there is only one of you, so nobody with a mouth will ever pronounce it. But she is going to need to call you something.”

  • Tzuriel God is my rock
  • Chesediel the mercy of God
  • Nuriel the fire of God
  • Shomriel God keeps watch
  • Something else free text
Norm handled in fictionThe Directory on Popular Piety §217 discourages assigning names to angels other than the three in Scripture. In-story, the name is not yours — it is only what she will one day call you, and she will not hear the true one until scene 12. Cosmetic everywhere except one line at the end.
1.12The commission

“Her name is Ruth Kowalczyk. She will be born in four hours, in Pittsburgh, to a mother who is nineteen and frightened. You will be there before the doctor is. That is the whole of your commission. Everything else you work out yourself.” You wait for more. There is no more.

1.13One question

You are allowed to ask one thing about her before you go. This is the whole of character customisation, and it is an act of character rather than a menu: your first decision as a guardian is what you want to know in advance.

  • “What will she suffer?” the shape, not the timing
  • “Whom will she love?” the names, not the outcomes
  • “What will she be good at?” her gift — and sixty years of watching her not use it
  • “Nothing. I’ll find out.” charity %+25 · real advantage in 11
Cost: one flag, zero forked proseIt changes how the player reads all of scene 3 without duplicating a line of it. The ward is never chosen — you do not shop for a charge. That is the premise.
SCENE TWO

Four Hours

1953 · Magee-Womens Hospital, and a house on Dobson Street
Words 1,050Choices 4Images 1

Teach the limits by making the player run into every one of them in ten minutes. You cannot touch her, cannot be seen, cannot be heard, cannot read her, and cannot leave.

2.1Before the doctor

What an angel actually sees looking at a person for the first time — and, immediately, what it does not. Every fact about her outside; nothing at all about her inside. A player who has just spent a whole scene handing thoughts back and forth instantly feels the wall.

2.2What you can do

Discovered by trying, not by exposition. You can move small things a little. You can put a thought in front of a person — never into them. You can be somewhere. That is the toolkit for seventy years.

  • First act. Whatever the player reaches for teaches them the size of the toolkit by failing at three-quarters of it.
2.3Dana, and the corridor

Nineteen, unmarried, in 1953, in a Catholic hospital in a Polish neighbourhood. Terry is not there. Halina is in the corridor working a rosary and has not said a word to her daughter in four months. Nobody in this building is happy, and a child is about to be born into it, and that is normal.

2.4The other one

Across the room, something else is also here. It does not speak. It looks at you, and looks away, and gets to work. It will not say a word to you for eleven years.

  • Whatever you do here, it does not respond. That is the point — and it makes 3.4 land.
2.5St. CasimirIMAGE 2

Three weeks later. What actually happens at a baptism, told plainly and without sentiment — and what you watch happen that nobody in the building can see. Halina is the one holding her, because Dana’s hands are shaking.

  • Charity fork. The first time the player is offered the chance to make a good moment slightly more about themselves.
Sets the Prayers resourceHalina starts praying for Ruth by name, daily, this week. She does not stop for fifteen years. The player will not notice this number until 3.8, which is exactly when they are supposed to.
2.6The first night

The house on Dobson Street. Three generations, four rooms, one furnace. You have nothing to do and nowhere else to be, and you discover that both of those are permanent.

SCENE THREE

Ordinary Time

1958–1970 · ages five to seventeen
Words 6,500Choices 33Images 3

The scene the whole game is decided by. Twelve years of an unremarkable childhood, no cosmic scenery, and the player has to stay gripped by reading a girl. If this works, the game works.

3.1The first thing she chooses1958 · 5

She takes something small that is not hers, from a counter on Liberty Avenue, and she knows she is doing it. The first act you can call chosen rather than merely done. You have never wanted anything before and now you want her to put it back, and you find out what wanting is worth.

  • Put the shopkeeper’s eyes on her. severity %+15
  • Put Halina’s face in her head. mercy %+10 · charity %+10
  • Nothing. Let it be hers. patience %+20
Teaches the ceilingWhatever the player picks, she keeps it, because none of the three tools reaches inside. This is the lesson and it should sting a little.
3.2Halina1960 · 7

First confession, first communion. Halina teaches her, in Polish and bad English, and the teaching is working — and it is also, in two specific places, wrong. Your temptation is to improve on it. Every correction you make is one more thing that is yours instead of hers.

  • Correct the theology. Arrange for a sister to say it properly. severity %+15 · overreach +1
  • Leave it. Halina is giving her something better than accuracy. patience %+20 · charity %+15
  • Strengthen only what is already right. mercy %+15 · splendor %−10
Loaded gunOne of the two wrong things Halina teaches her is the fate of unbaptised infants. It goes off in 1981.
3.3Three days1962 · 9

Tuesday, October, kitchen light on at four. She comes home and does not speak. Not sulking — she nods, eats, does her homework, goes to bed. Wednesday the same. Thursday the same. You have a perfect file on her outside and no access at all to her inside, and she is nine and something is wrong and you are standing four feet away and there is no way to ask.

What you have: eleven hand-washings on Tuesday night. The third row of the bus instead of the back, for the first time in two years. Bernie absent Monday and Tuesday. An 88 on a maths test buried in her bag. And, Tuesday night with the door shut, a prayer said alone for the first time in her life — four words, and then she stopped and could not finish it.

  • Somebody did something to her. Find out who. valor %+15 · severity %+10
  • It’s the test. She has decided she is stupid. mercy %+15 · severity %−10
  • It’s Bernie. She thinks her friend is gone. charity %+15
  • You will not work this out. Stay. Be close. Wait until she comes to it. patience %+25 · charity %+20 · splendor %−15
What it actually isTwo Sundays ago a thought came into her head during the consecration that she believes is the worst thing anyone has ever thought. She believes she committed a mortal sin, that she cannot receive, and that she cannot tell anyone — because saying it out loud would be saying it again. So she has stopped talking. Literally. To keep it from coming out of her mouth.
Why the angel cannot see itEvery fact in the file reads as devotion. He watches a nine-year-old start praying alone for the first time in her life and scores it good. His perfect external data is not merely insufficient here — it is actively pointing the wrong way, and that is the cruellest and truest thing in the game.
OutcomesEach branch gets a small, immediate, legible consequence. Hunt for it and she shuts down harder and stays shut through the weekend. Send her mother to the bag and she learns her bag is not private. Chase Bernie and you learn it was never Bernie, which is worth something. Stay, and on Thursday night, asleep, she rolls toward where you are — nothing in her knows, and something in her settles, and she sleeps until six. The staying option is right and the game never says so.
3.4Rudy1964 · 11

Eleven years and it has never spoken. You pass each other the way two men pass in the same stairwell every morning for a decade without saying good morning. Then in November, outside the school, it comes and stands next to you, facing the same way you are facing, the way two men at a bar look at the bottles instead of at each other. “So how’s it going.”

It is tired, chatty, professional, and not remotely threatening, which is worse. Its argument: your job is harder than mine and nobody says so. I need one thing — I need her to do a thing she already wants to do, enough times that she stops noticing she is doing it. I am not building anything. You are building something. Every word true, handed over openly, because it has correctly worked out that truth is the most discouraging thing it owns. It offers a name the way a man offers a business card. Rudy.

  • Say nothing. Not now, not in sixty years. patience %+20 · splendor %−20
  • “You’re wrong about one thing.” splendor %+20 · valor %+10 · sets caught_the_lie
  • “Go away from her.” severity %+25 · valor %+20
  • Ask it a question. mercy %+10 · opens scene 9
The crack in itIt says it is not building anything. It is. It is building a habit, and a habit is a structure, and it has been laying that brick for eleven years. It is not lying to you — it cannot — it is lying to itself, which is the only kind of lying left to something that cannot lie to a spirit. Catching that matters in 11.
3.5Bernie1965 · 12

Bernadette Sobek. The friendship is the best thing in Ruth’s life and it is quietly costing Bernie something, and neither girl can see it. You can. So can somebody else, standing on the other side of it, who is not your enemy.

  • Three options, all of which are defensible, none of which is free. Sets up scene 4.
3.6What she did to Amanda Pyle1966 · 13

Deliberate, sustained, and inventive cruelty to a girl who could not defend herself. It works. She gets away with it. She likes it. And her conscience — the same conscience that put her in the ground for three days over a thought she did not choose — does not make one sound.

  • Bring it down on her. Now, hard, while she can still feel it. severity %+25 · valor %+15
  • Put Amanda in front of her until she has to look. severity %+10 · charity %+15
  • Go to Amanda instead. She has her own, but two is not too many. charity %+25 · splendor %−15 · overreach +1

The diagnostic beatPlaced four beats after 3.3 so the contrast is unmissable: scrupulosity is not a strong conscience, it is a broken instrument — deafening about what is not there and silent about what is. The player works this out before the angel does, and that is the first time the player is ahead.
3.7The Mass changes1967 · 14

The altar turns around. The Latin goes. Halina, who has prayed in Latin and Polish for sixty years and reads no English, is left standing in a building she no longer recognises, holding a rosary through a Mass she is not supposed to need it for. You know the Church has the authority to do this. You know it is legitimate. You are watching it cost one specific old woman nearly everything, and you cannot explain either half to anybody. Ruth watches her grandmother and draws a conclusion about God, and you cannot argue with a conclusion.

  • Three options. No villain in this beat and no polemic in any direction — the reform is real, the loss is real, and both are allowed to be true.
3.8Halina1968 · 15

She dies in February, in the front room, quickly. And the Prayers number — which has sat in the corner of the stat panel for fifteen years, high and steady and completely unremarked — falls to almost nothing on screen while the player is reading the sentence about the funeral.

Grief delivered as a statNo text points at the number. The player either notices or does not, and the ones who notice will remember it longer than anything written.
3.9The 54 Flyer1969 · 16

A stranger on a bus is kind to her for ninety seconds, for no reason, about nothing, and she cries at the window for four stops and gets off better than she got on. You had nothing to do with any of it. You did not arrange the stranger. You did not know it was coming. Grace has just operated in your ward at close range and completely around you, and you have to decide what you are.

  • Find out who that was and thank them. charity %+15
  • Note the method. Use it yourself next time. severity %+10 · splendor %+10
  • Nothing. It was not yours and it was not for you. charity %+25 · patience %+15
Humility beatThe one place the game says outright: you are not the mechanism. It is placed immediately before the parking lot so the player enters the worst hour of the scene having just been told they are not in charge.
3.10The parking lot1970 · 17

Sharpsburg, behind a bar, two in the morning, and a boy, and a car, and a decision that is grave matter in a way nothing yet has been. You have three tools, all of them small, and about eleven minutes. Scene ends on the knife.

  • Three options, and every one of them is inadequate, and the player is meant to feel that clearly.
SCENE FOUR

The Other Watch

1970 · the same week
Words 2,250Choices 6

The first conversation you have had with your own kind since the court. And it is an argument, with somebody who is not wrong.

4.1Bernie’s

Older than you, in the sense that matters, and it has been standing in the same rooms for five years without either of you saying anything. It opens by handing you something — instantly, entire, no preamble — and it is a fact about Bernie that you did not have and did not want.

4.2Two goods

What Bernie needs in order to survive the next four years requires Ruth to lose something she cannot replace. Neither angel is wrong. Neither can be overruled. There is no arbitration available and no time to ask for any. You are both under obedience and you are both obeying, and you are on opposite sides of one afternoon.

  • Six choices across the scene. Every one is a negotiation between two beings who cannot lie to each other, which makes it a very strange and very clean kind of argument.
What this scene is forIt is the only place in the game that shows the reader what the communion of the angels is actually like — and it is not harmony. It is thousands of separate commissions, all good, colliding constantly, resolved by nobody in particular, in a providence none of them can see the whole of. That is a truer picture than a choir.

SCENE SIX

Twenty-One Days

1970 · eleven minutes, in real time

The parking lot resolves, and the three verbs come back. The player recognises the shape from scene 1 and that recognition is the payoff of putting a battle in a creation story.

6.1What Rudy is standing on

Not the boy. Not the car. Rudy is standing on a sentence Ruth said to herself in 1962, and everything in this parking lot is downstream of it. You have to get through to it, and Rudy is there, and neither of you can hurt the other.

6.2The lock

You PRESS and it costs you both. You are locked. The clock on screen is Ruth walking across a parking lot, and it does not stop while you are locked. This is Daniel 10 with the twenty-one days compressed into eleven minutes, and it is the only kind of urgency the theology actually permits.

SCENE SEVEN

SCENE EIGHT

What She Asks

1981 · and the eight years after
Words 1,900Choices 7Images 1

The hardest scene to write and the one the game exists for. The gun Halina loaded in 1960 goes off.

8.1Halina Dolan1981 · 28

Stillborn, at thirty-six weeks, named for her great-grandmother. No baptism. There are no other children afterwards, and no reason given for that either.

8.2The other angel

There is one, and it is standing in the room, and its commission is already over. It had a few months and it did them, and there is nothing further assigned to it, ever. It is not sad. You cannot work out what it is, and it will not hand it over, and you will think about that for forty years.

Handle with total restraintNo speech from it, no explanation, no consolation offered to the reader. It is present, its work is complete, and the scene moves on. Anything more turns a mystery into a doctrine we do not have.
8.3What she believes happened

Ruth was formed on the catechesis of 1960 by a woman born in 1898. She believes her daughter is in Limbo, that she will never see her, and that it is her own fault. None of that was ever the teaching of the Church. Limbo was a theological opinion, never defined; the Church has never said where that child is and has always said we may hope. And you cannot tell her, because you are not a person, and the only way this gets fixed is if some human being who knows says it out loud to her face — and the ones around her do not know either.

  • Arrange it gently and let it fail if it fails. patience %+15 · mercy %+15

Why this is the centre of the gameIt is a real ethical dilemma with no clean answer, drawn entirely from real Catholic theology, and it is not propaganda in any direction — the woman is wrong, her teachers meant well, the Church never taught the thing that is destroying her, and no one available can tell her so. That is a tragedy of catechesis, and it happened to a hundred thousand real women.
8.4The prayer

She asks for something. You carry prayers — that is a real part of the office and this is the first time the game has said so. And what she is asking for, if she got it, would finish her. Do you carry it as spoken?

  • Three options. The correct answer is obvious and the game makes obeying it feel like a betrayal, which is the point.

Your job in this beatAlso to stay. The scene deliberately gives the player almost nothing to do, and the ones who find that unbearable are being taught something.
