One Soul

You are an angel, and one life is yours to keep.

Target 24,400 words Choices 154 Scenes 12 Ward Adam Kowalczyk, 1975–2024

Draft 2 replaces the ward and the whole middle of the story. It is now a man’s life, and the trial is chastity — approached through scrupulosity, argued away at eighteen, and paid for over thirty years. Everything mechanical from draft 1 survives. The prose is still bracketed.

00The four rules that keep this from becoming a tract

This material wants to collapse into a testimony pamphlet — boy sins, boy rationalises, boy falls, boy returns — and a reader who is not already convinced will smell the moral coming three scenes out and stop trusting the narrator. Four structural rules stop that, and they are not negotiable once writing begins.

Rule one · his guilt is never established

Mortal sin needs grave matter, full knowledge, and deliberate consent (CCC 1857). Consent lives in the interior, and no creature can see in there — not the angel, not the demons, not the player. And CCC 2352, on this act specifically, says affective immaturity, force of habit, and conditions of anxiety can lessen culpability or reduce it to a minimum. So the ward’s column carries a field that reads culpability: unknown from scene 2 to scene 12 and never changes once, for seventy years. The game explains it plainly, early, and then makes the player look at it for the rest of their life.

Rule two · the angel never touches the will

Not once, on any branch. Every tool the angel has works on circumstances: put a person in his path, put a book on a table, stand on a suggestion so it does not reach him, be somewhere. If a single beat is ever written where the angel moves Adam’s choice from inside, the whole game becomes a puppet show and the theology is gone.

Rule three · no plot punishment

Nothing bad happens to Adam because he did something wrong. No accidents, no illnesses, no ruin arriving as a verdict. Every consequence in this game is intrinsic — the act doing what the act does, visible in how he treats one specific woman. That distinction is the entire thesis: the teaching is not a rule with a penalty attached from outside, it is a description of what a person is, and breaking it damages you the way walking into a wall damages you.

Rule four · the strongest version of every argument

Adam’s question at eighteen — why would God give me this and then forbid it? — gets written at full strength, because a strawman is worth nothing. Nietzsche’s critique gets written at full strength too. The game wins nothing by argument. It wins by showing what happens to one man over thirty years, and letting the reader do the rest.

01The thesis, rendered as a needle

The game never says that the teaching exists for his flourishing. Instead there is a reading in his column with four positions, and the whole story is the angel trying to move it and mostly failing.

1
A rule.It is forbidden. That is all he knows and it is all anyone has told him.1982 — age 7
2
A rule about dirt.It is shameful, and therefore so is he. Arrives via one brusque confessor and one embarrassed father, neither of whom meant it.1990 — age 15
3
A rule against me.God built this into me and then said no. He argues himself here at eighteen and stays for twenty-five years.1993–2018 — ages 18 to 43
4
Not a rule at all.A description of what a person is for — and therefore of what it costs to use one. Reachable only at the very end, partially, and not on every branch.2018 — age 43, if at all

Nobody in the game ever states position 4 as a thesis. A competent confessor says it once, badly compressed, in 2018, thirty years too late. The rest of the argument is made by watching one woman’s face across a kitchen table in 1997.

02The stat sheet

Unchanged from draft 1 — three opposed pairs and one free-standing virtue. Severity and Mercy carry more weight in this version than in the last, because nearly every dilemma in the middle of the game is a question about how to stand next to somebody who is failing.

ValorPatience

Move now, or keep the long watch. One slider, two names — high Valor is low Patience.

SeverityMercy

How you stand next to a failure. Both are real virtues. Neither is the answer, and in this game the wrong one at the wrong moment does thirty years of damage.

03Legion

One named devil is cosy. This one is a committee, and the committee is a bluff.

They present as many. Different names on different days, different voices, handoffs, that’s not my area, you want the other one. They borrow big names — Belphegor, Asmodeus, Mammon — and none of them is entitled to any of them. The angel spends nineteen years unable to get a grip on what he is facing, which is exactly the intended effect.

“My name is Legion: for we are many.”

Mark 5:9 — a boast, and quite possibly a lie, right there in the Gospel

In 1994 the angel counts them. There are three. One does nearly all the work; the other two exist mostly to make it look like a crowd. And the discovery is not just a plot beat — it is the game’s method in miniature. Complication is the tactic. The truth stays the same size no matter how many voices are talking over it.

It rhymes with what is happening to Adam: his justifications get more elaborate every decade, and the thing they are justifying never changes at all.

05Five threads

Scrupulosity — 3.4 → 6.2 → 6.4 → 11.6 The needle — 3.3 → 6.3 → 6.6 → 11.4 Renee — 3.6 → 4 → 8 → 9.4 → 11.5 Legion — 2.4 → 7 → 10.3 → 11.6 Stan’s list — 6.5 → 10.1 → 11.1

Scrupulosity comes first and it is what makes the chastity trial land the way it does. A boy who at nine is pointing his finger at the ground forty times a day to undo an accident meets, at thirteen, a matter where the Church actually does say grave. His conscience was already a broken instrument. Now it has something real to work on, and it tears him apart over four years, and the tearing is what generates the argument — not the pleasure. He does not argue his way out because he wants to. He argues his way out because he cannot live inside what he has been told, and nobody has told him the true version.

And the machine is deaf in the other direction, exactly as it was in draft 1. In 1998 he does something to Renee that is genuinely cruel, deliberate, and defensible-sounding, and his conscience — the same conscience that put him in a confessional line every Saturday for four years — makes no sound at all.

06The twelve scenes

Each panel’s left edge names the stat pair the scene mainly exercises — Valor/Patience, Severity/Mercy, Splendor/Hiddenness, Charity. Reading down the edges: two, three, two, three. Balanced, which was not guaranteed — an earlier pass had five ruby scenes in a row and would have made Mercy the only stat that mattered.

SCENE ONE

The Assembly

Before there was anything to date it by
Words 1,900Choices 22Images 2

Unchanged from draft 1. Character creation disguised as the only decision that ever mattered.

1.1–1.3You are · the throne · the court

Creation, whole and instant. The retrospective frame stated flatly on page one — you are remembering this from the far end of a human life, which is why the story keeps reaching for human comparisons you do not have yet. One image only: a throne, fire that burns nothing, a floor like ice with light coming up through it. Then the court, and the speech rule in plain words: hand a thing over and the other knows it entire; nothing garbles, nothing can be faked. Keep it, or give it. And everyone can tell which you did.

1.4One of them shuts

The first sin announces itself as a silence. A mind ahead of you turns its back and stops letting anyone in, and every mind in the room feels the door close at once.

  • Go toward it now, before it spreads. valor %+20
  • Hold. You will not move until you know what you are moving against. valor %−20 · severity %+10
  • Speak. Ask the court out loud what has happened. splendor %+20
  • Turn back to the throne and stay there. charity %+15 · splendor %−10
1.5–1.7The vision · his reading · four wordsIMAGE 1

Full bleed, no caption: a room, early, grey light, a girl on the edge of a bed with the covers shoved around, barefoot, hair not done, and a light standing upright in the corner that is not a lamp. Nobody explains it. You are shown one thing and asked to say yes without being told what it means. Then he turns around and explains it, and every word is true, and what he leaves out is the only thing that matters — and that is good — which he can leave out because it is not a fact but a judgement. 1.8–1.10The expulsion · the ordersTUTORIAL

Ninety seconds, three choices, all three verbs: STAND on a mind that has not decided, get PRESSED and locked with something older and brighter that cannot hurt you and cannot be moved, SHOW it something to get free. Not to a nation, not to a Church, not to an army. To one person. One. Each.

“You have a name. You won’t use it here and neither will I — it isn’t a word, it’s the thing you are.   • “Whom will he love?” the names, not the outcomes

SCENE TWO

Four Hours

1975 · Magee-Womens Hospital, and a house on Dobson Street
Words 900Choices 4Images 1

Teach every limit by making the player hit all of them in ten minutes.

2.1–2.2Before the doctor · what you can do

You see every fact about his outside and nothing whatsoever about his inside. A player who has just spent a whole scene handing thoughts back and forth feels that wall immediately. The toolkit, discovered by trying rather than by exposition: you can move small things a little, you can put a thought in front of a person — never into them — and you can be somewhere. That is the whole toolkit for forty-nine years.

2.3culpability: unknown

The stat panel opens for the first time and there is a field on it that reads unknown. The game explains, once, in about sixty words, why it will never say anything else: knowing whether a person is guilty requires seeing what they knew and what they consented to, and there is exactly one Person who can do that, and you are not Him. Then it never mentions the field again, and the player looks at it for the rest of the game.

2.4The others

Across the room, something else is also here. Then you notice a second one. Then you are not sure whether the second one is a second one. None of them speaks to you and you cannot get a count. This is the first appearance of the only tactic they have and you will not understand it for nineteen years.

2.5–2.6St. Casimir · the first nightIMAGE 2

The baptism, told plainly and without sentiment, and what you watch happen that nobody in the building can see. Halina holds him. She begins praying for him by name this week and does not stop for eleven years — and that is where the Prayers number in the corner of the panel comes from, which the player will not think about at all until 1986.

3.4The finger1984 · 9

Reaching for a glass at the kitchen table, his hand opens wrong and his middle finger goes up on its own for about a quarter of a second. He puts the glass down. Then he points the finger at the floor, deliberately, to reverse it — because he made the sign, and making the sign is a sin, and if he does not undo it he will have to confess it, and what if he forgets, and what if he forgets and then receives.

He does this forty times a day for four years. Nobody in the house notices. He is careful about that.

And here is what you see: a nine-year-old boy who is extremely serious about sin. Which is the reading any angel would make, because it is the only reading available from outside. You are watching an instrument break and calling it piety.

3.5The mill1985 · 10

It closes. Stan is thirty-eight and has been there since he was nineteen and will never have work like it again. Half the street goes. Adam learns, at ten, that the thing that holds a life up can be taken away in a single announcement — and that his father will not say one word about it, ever, to anyone.

3.6Renee1984–88

Renee Sobek, three doors down, same age. They are nine and then they are twelve and then they are thirteen. There is nothing romantic about any of it yet and that matters enormously: the game needs the player to like her, at length, as a person, before she is ever anything else. Her angel is around and the two of you nod and that is all, for now.

3.7Halina1986 · 11

She dies in February, in the back room, over about a week. And the Prayers number — sitting high and steady in the corner of the stat panel for eleven years, entirely unremarked — falls to almost nothing on screen while the player is reading the sentence about the funeral.

No text points at the number.The player either notices or does not, and the ones who notice will remember it longer than anything written.
3.8The classroom door1988 · 13

A health class. A filmstrip. A teacher who would rather be anywhere else. Something is described clinically, as a fact about bodies, with no moral frame attached to it whatsoever — which is not malice, it is a public school in 1988.

He comes out. He is quiet on the walk home. He is quiet at dinner. That is everything you can see, because that is everything there is to see.

The player, who is a human being and has been thirteen, knows precisely what has happened. You do not. And from this page to the end of the game, the player is ahead of you.

This solves the content problem completelyThe narration cannot go inside, ever, because the narrator cannot. A door, a light, a boy who does not meet his mother’s eye the next morning. That is the register for the rest of the game and it is not a euphemism — it is the actual epistemic situation of a guardian angel.
Scene ends here.On a boy walking home, and an angel who does not know why.
SCENE FOUR

The Other Watch

1988 · the same autumn
Words 1,400Choices 6

Your first real conversation with your own kind since the court — and it is an argument with somebody who is not wrong.

4.1–4.2Renee’s

Four years of standing in the same rooms without saying anything. It opens by handing you something — instantly, entire, no preamble — a fact about Renee that you did not have and did not want. And then the problem: what Renee needs in order to get through the next four years requires Adam to lose something he cannot replace. Neither of you is wrong. Neither can overrule the other. There is no arbitration and no time to ask for any.

What this scene is forIt is the only place that shows what the communion of angels is actually like, and it is not harmony. It is thousands of separate commissions, all good, colliding constantly, resolved by nobody in particular, inside a providence none of them can see the whole of. That is truer than a choir — and it is the first time the player understands that “good” is not a single quantity everyone is maximising.

This is the wound. Not a bad father. A silent one, who was raised by a silent one, and who will spend twenty-three years praying about the thing he could not say out loud once.

  • Four choices, and the good one is to work on Stan, which most players will not think of because Stan is not the ward.
6.5The argument1993 · 18

It arrives fully formed, on a bus, and it is good: Why would He build this into me at thirteen, with no say from me, and then tell me it is mortal? Either He is cruel or the rule is man-made, and I do not think He is cruel.

Written at full strength. No strawman. It has convinced a very large number of people and it convinces Adam in about four seconds, because he has been looking for it for four years.

And you know the answer. You have known it since before there were bodies. The answer is that I am able to was never an argument for I should — he is also able to lie, to eat until he is sick, to use a person and call it affection — and that the rule is not a fence around the body but a description of what a person is, and therefore of what it costs to treat one as a thing.

And you cannot say it. You have three tools and none of them is a mouth. And the only human being in Pittsburgh who could put it to him properly is a man he has already decided is disgusted by him.

  • Find someone who can say it. Spend everything. valor %+25 · overreach +2 · low odds, enormous payoff
  • Put a book in front of him. splendor %−15 · patience %+15 · he will not read it for twenty-five years
  • Work on Renee instead. She is about to matter. charity %+20
  • Let the argument stand. Arguing back is not your office. patience %+30 · severity %+10
The needle moves to 3 on every branch.It has to. This is the fixed spine and the player cannot prevent it. What they can change is how long he stays there and what it costs on the way.
6.6He stops going1993

Not dramatically. There is no scene, no argument with his mother, no slammed door. SCENE SEVEN

Legion

1994 · a dorm corridor in Oakland
Words 900Choices 8

The one clean win in the game, and it is a win by counting.

7.1The committee

Nineteen years of this. One introduces itself as Belphegor and hands you off. Another says that is not its area. A third takes a message. One claims to be Asmodeus, which would be a serious matter if it were true. Eight fast choices. Not a fight — a piece of arithmetic, done by STANDING on the same thing twice and seeing who turns up.

The method of the whole game, in one sceneComplication is the tactic. The truth is always smaller than the noise around it — and it rhymes with what is happening to Adam, whose justifications get more elaborate every decade while the thing they are justifying never changes size at all.

SCENE EIGHT

What He Doesn’t Tell Her

Words 2,200Choices 14Images 1

The hinge of the entire game. Everything before it is setup and everything after it is consequence.

8.1The scale changes

A modem in a shared apartment on Semple Street. What used to require going somewhere and being seen now requires nothing at all. Rendered entirely as hours and light — when he sleeps, when he stops sleeping, what a room looks like at four in the morning.

8.2She nearly finds out

Tell her, or don’t.

  • Arrange for him to tell her. charity %+25 · valor %+15
  • Arrange for her to find out. severity %+25 · overreach +2
  • Let him keep it. patience %+15 · the needle stays at 3 and something else begins
  • Work on Renee’s angel. Ask what it wants. charity %+20 · and it wants something you will not like
Telling her is not a magic fix and the game must not pretend it isShe does not understand. She is hurt, and confused, and she handles it imperfectly because she is twenty-one. It costs them badly and they may not survive it. What it does is keep him a person to himself — and that is the only thing on offer, and the game has to be honest that it is a small thing bought at a large price.
8.3What concealment actually does

If he keeps it, the damage is not the distance. The damage is the hierarchy.

He knows something she does not. He has looked at what people actually are and she has not. He is honest with himself; she lives in something comfortable. He is free and she is naive — and every week he does not tell her, that gap widens, and something moves in to fill it.

The contempt arrives years before the philosophy does. That is the most important sentence in this bible.

Rendered, never statedHe starts finishing her sentences. He starts explaining things to her she already knows. He becomes patient with her in a way that is not kindness. Nobody in the story ever uses the word contempt.
8.4The kitchen table1997

She asks him what is wrong. He says nothing is wrong. And he is telling the truth as he understands it, which is the loneliest thing in the game, and the player can see both sides of the table and neither of them can.

Two angels in a small kitchen watching two people who have known each other since they were nine become strangers, four feet apart, over about eleven months.

This is the argumentNot a homily, not a priest, not a paragraph of explanation. One woman’s face across a table. If the reader does not understand from this beat what the teaching is protecting, no amount of theology later will fix it — and if they do, no theology is needed.
SCENE NINE

The Permission Slip

1997–1998 · ages twenty-two and twenty-three
Words 1,600Choices 10

He does not read his way into contempt. He is already contemptuous and goes looking for something that dignifies it.

9.1Forty pages

A course he is not enrolled in, a paperback somebody left, a conversation in a bar with a graduate student. The critique is written at full strength, because a strawman is worth nothing: that Christian morality is a revolt of the weak, that it takes an inability to act and renames it virtue, that pity and chastity and humility are what people call their limitations when they want to be admired for them.

It is a serious argument. It has convinced serious people. And Adam does not engage with a single word of it. He reads about forty pages, takes away one sentence’s worth of attitude, and never opens it again.

Why this is more damning than a refutationThe game does not need to beat Nietzsche. It needs to show that Adam was never argued into anything. He acquired a posture and then went shopping for a reason, and found one so good that he did not have to think again for twenty years.
9.2What Legion does with it

Almost nothing, and that is the point — the beat where the player realises how little the demons have had to do. They did not put the book there. They did not construct the argument. 12.2The name

He sees you. For the first time in forty-nine years. And he says your name — the real one, the one that is not a word and that nobody with a mouth can pronounce — because he can now, and because knowing it was never the problem.

12.3The window goes dark

The game ends before anything is said. God has no dialogue in this game and does not get any in the last scene either. The commission ends at the threshold and so does the story.

12.4What you have to hope on

No ending of this game ever says that Adam was damned. Not one, on any branch. The Church has never in two thousand years declared a single individual to be in hell — she canonises and has never once done the reverse — and this game is not going to be the first thing to do it.

What the endings differ in is how much there is to hope on: where the needle finished, whether he asked for a priest, what the last thing he said was, how much wreckage he left and whether any of it got repaired, whether Renee ever heard from him again, and what kind of angel forty-nine years made of you — because the reading is yours, and you have never been the judge of anything.

And the worst ending does not say he is lost. It says something considerably harder to sit with: you do not know, you will not know until the resurrection, and you are going to hope anyway, for all of it. Hope is a virtue, not a guess. That distinction is the last thing the game teaches and it does not explain it.

That chart is the only verdict the player is ever given, and it is not a verdict on him.
