#! Council of Trent, Session 13 (11 October 1551), Decree on the Most Holy Eucharist — translated for Lumina from the Latin.

# Proem
[Proem] The holy, ecumenical and general Council of Trent, lawfully assembled in the Holy Spirit, with the same legate and nuncios of the holy Roman Church presiding, has met, not without the special guidance and direction of the Holy Spirit, to set out the true and ancient teaching on faith and the sacraments, and to bring a remedy for all the heresies and other very serious troubles by which the Church of God is now miserably shaken and torn into many different parts. From the very beginning it has especially wanted to pull up by the roots the weeds of hateful errors and divisions that the enemy has sown, in these unhappy times of ours, in the teaching of faith about the most holy Eucharist and in its use and worship (Matthew 13:25). Yet our Saviour left the Eucharist in his Church as a sign of the unity and love by which he willed all Christians to be joined and bound together. So this same holy Council hands on the sound and pure teaching about this venerable and divine sacrament of the Eucharist which the Catholic Church, instructed by our Lord Jesus Christ himself and his apostles, and taught by the Holy Spirit, who reminds her day by day of all truth (John 14:26), has always kept and will keep until the end of the age. It forbids all Christ's faithful from now on to dare to believe, teach or preach about the most holy Eucharist otherwise than is explained and defined in this present decree.

# Chapter 1 · The real presence of our Lord Jesus Christ in the most holy sacrament of the Eucharist
[1] To begin with, the holy Council teaches, and openly and plainly professes, that in the nourishing sacrament of the holy Eucharist, after the consecration of the bread and wine, our Lord Jesus Christ, true God and true man, is truly, really and substantially contained under the appearance of those things that can be perceived by the senses. There is no contradiction between our Saviour always sitting at the right hand of the Father in heaven, in his natural way of existing, and his being present to us by his substance, sacramentally, in many other places, in a way of existing which we can hardly put into words, but which our thinking, enlightened by faith, can recognize as possible for God, and which we must believe most firmly.
+ For all our forebears in the true Church of Christ who have written about this most holy sacrament have professed most plainly that our Redeemer instituted this wonderful sacrament at the Last Supper, when, after blessing the bread and wine, he declared in clear and distinct words that he was giving them his own body and his own blood. These words are recorded by the holy evangelists and repeated afterwards by St Paul (Matthew 26:26–28; Mark 14:22–24; Luke 22:19–20; 1 Corinthians 11:23–25). Since they carry their proper and most obvious meaning, the meaning in which the Fathers understood them, it is a shameful outrage that some quarrelsome and wicked people twist them, against the whole mind of the Church, into made-up and imaginary figures of speech that deny the reality of Christ's flesh and blood. The Church, "the pillar and bulwark of the truth" (1 Timothy 3:15), has rejected these inventions of ungodly people as the work of Satan, always recognizing this supreme gift of Christ with a grateful and remembering heart.

# Chapter 2 · Why this most holy sacrament was instituted
[2] So our Saviour, when he was about to leave this world and go to the Father, instituted this sacrament. In it he poured out, as it were, the riches of his divine love for humanity, "making a memorial of his wonderful works" (Psalm 111:4), and he commanded us, when we receive it, to cherish his memory (1 Corinthians 11:24) and to proclaim his death until he comes (1 Corinthians 11:26) to judge the world.
+ He willed this sacrament to be received as the spiritual food of souls (Matthew 26:26), to nourish and strengthen those who live with the life of him who said: "Whoever eats me will live because of me" (John 6:57). He willed it also as a remedy, to free us from our daily faults and to keep us from mortal sins. He willed it, further, to be a pledge of our future glory and everlasting happiness, and so a sign of that one body of which he himself is the head (1 Corinthians 11:3; Ephesians 5:23), and to which he willed us, as its members, to be bound by the closest ties of faith, hope and love, so that we might all say the same thing and there might be no divisions among us (1 Corinthians 1:10).

# Chapter 3 · The excellence of the most holy Eucharist above the other sacraments
[3] The most holy Eucharist has this in common with the other sacraments: it is a sign of a sacred thing, and the visible form of an invisible grace. But this excellent and unique thing is found in it: the other sacraments have the power to make holy only when someone uses them, but in the Eucharist the very author of holiness is there before it is used. The apostles had not yet received the Eucharist from the Lord's hand (Matthew 26:26; Mark 14:22), and yet he himself was saying truly that what he was giving them was his body.
+ And this has always been the faith in the Church of God: that immediately after the consecration the true body of our Lord and his true blood exist under the appearance of bread and wine, together with his soul and divinity. The body is under the appearance of bread, and the blood under the appearance of wine, by the power of the words. But the body itself is also under the appearance of wine, and the blood under the appearance of bread, and the soul under both, by the force of that natural connection and accompaniment by which the parts of Christ the Lord, who has now risen from the dead never to die again (Romans 6:9), are joined together. And the divinity is there because of its wonderful hypostatic union with his body and soul. So it is most true that just as much is contained under either appearance as under both. For Christ, whole and entire, exists under the appearance of bread and under any part of that appearance, and likewise whole under the appearance of wine and under its parts.

# Chapter 4 · Transubstantiation
[4] Because Christ our Redeemer said that what he was offering under the appearance of bread was truly his body (Matthew 26:26–29; Mark 14:22–25; Luke 22:19–20; 1 Corinthians 11:24–26), the Church of God has always been convinced, and this holy Council now declares again, that by the consecration of the bread and wine there takes place a change of the whole substance of the bread into the substance of the body of Christ our Lord, and of the whole substance of the wine into the substance of his blood. This change the holy Catholic Church has fittingly and properly called transubstantiation.

# Chapter 5 · The worship and reverence to be shown to this most holy sacrament
[5] So there is no room left for doubt that all Christ's faithful, following the custom always received in the Catholic Church, are to give this most holy sacrament the worship of adoration (latria) which is due to the true God. It is not to be adored any less because Christ the Lord instituted it to be received (Matthew 26:26). For we believe that the same God is present in it whom the eternal Father, when he brought him into the world, spoke of, saying: "Let all God's angels worship him" (Hebrews 1:6); whom the Magi fell down and worshipped (Matthew 2:11); and whom, as Scripture testifies, the apostles worshipped in Galilee (Matthew 28:17).
+ The holy Council also declares that the custom was introduced into the Church of God with great devotion and reverence that every year, on a special feast day, this most high and venerable sacrament should be celebrated with special reverence and solemnity, and carried with reverence and honour in procession through the streets and public places. It is entirely right that some holy days should be set apart on which all Christians may show, in a special and unusual way, their grateful and remembering hearts towards their common Lord and Redeemer for so unspeakable and truly divine a gift, by which the victory and triumph of his death are shown forth. And so it was fitting that victorious truth should triumph over falsehood and heresy, so that its opponents, faced with such splendour and with such great joy of the whole Church, might either lose heart and fade away, broken, or, touched with shame and confusion, at last come to their senses.

# Chapter 6 · Reserving the sacrament of the holy Eucharist, and taking it to the sick
[6] The custom of reserving the holy Eucharist in a sacred place is so ancient that even the age of the Council of Nicaea knew it. Further, that the holy Eucharist itself should be taken to the sick, and carefully kept in churches for this purpose, is not only entirely fair and reasonable, but is also found commanded in many councils and has been observed by the most ancient custom of the Catholic Church. So this holy Council decrees that this saving and necessary custom is to be kept by all means.

# Chapter 7 · The preparation needed to receive the holy Eucharist worthily
[7] If it is not fitting for anyone to approach any sacred function except in a holy way, then certainly, the better a Christian understands the holiness and divinity of this heavenly sacrament, the more carefully they must take care not to come to receive it without great reverence and holiness; especially since we read in the Apostle those words full of dread: "Whoever eats and drinks unworthily, eats and drinks judgment upon themselves, not discerning the body of the Lord" (1 Corinthians 11:29). So whoever wishes to receive communion must be reminded of his command: "Let a person examine themselves" (1 Corinthians 11:28).
+ The custom of the Church declares that this examination is necessary: no one who is aware of being in mortal sin, however contrite they may seem to themselves, may approach the holy Eucharist without first making a sacramental confession. This holy Council has decreed that this must always be observed by all Christians, even by those priests whose office obliges them to celebrate, provided they have access to a confessor. But if a priest, under urgent necessity, celebrates without first confessing, he must confess as soon as possible.

# Chapter 8 · The use of this wonderful sacrament
[8] As to the use of this holy sacrament, our Fathers rightly and wisely distinguished three ways of receiving it. They taught that some receive it only sacramentally, as sinners do. Others receive it only spiritually: those who, eating in desire the heavenly bread set before them, with a living faith "working through love" (Galatians 5:6), experience its fruit and benefit. The third receive it both sacramentally and spiritually at once: these are the ones who first examine and prepare themselves so that they come to this divine table clothed in the wedding garment (Matthew 22:11–14).
+ In sacramental reception it has always been the custom in the Church of God that lay people receive communion from priests, and that priests who celebrate give communion to themselves. This custom, as coming down from apostolic tradition, ought rightly and deservedly to be kept.
+ Finally, the holy Council, with a father's love, warns, urges, asks and begs, "by the tender mercy of our God" (Luke 1:78), that each and every one who bears the name of Christian may now at last come together and be of one mind in this sign of unity, this bond of love, this symbol of harmony. Mindful of the great majesty and the surpassing love of our Lord Jesus Christ, who gave his beloved life as the price of our salvation, and gave us his flesh to eat (John 6:48–58), may they believe and revere these sacred mysteries of his body and blood with such constant and firm faith, such devotion of heart, such reverence and worship, that they can receive often that supersubstantial bread (Matthew 6:11). May it truly be for them the life of the soul and lasting health of mind; and strengthened by it (1 Kings 19:8), may they come from the journey of this sorrowful pilgrimage to their heavenly homeland, there to eat without any veil the same bread of angels (Psalm 78:25) that they now eat beneath the sacred veils.

# Canons on the Most Holy Sacrament of the Eucharist
[Canons] But since it is not enough to state the truth unless errors are exposed and refuted, it has pleased the holy Council to add these canons, so that all, now that they know the Catholic teaching, may also understand which heresies they must guard against and avoid.
[Can. 1] If anyone denies that in the sacrament of the most holy Eucharist there are contained truly, really and substantially the body and blood, together with the soul and divinity, of our Lord Jesus Christ, and so the whole Christ; but says that he is in it only as in a sign or figure, or by his power: let them be anathema.
[Can. 2] If anyone says that in the holy sacrament of the Eucharist the substance of the bread and wine remains together with the body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, and denies that wonderful and unique change of the whole substance of the bread into the body, and of the whole substance of the wine into the blood, while only the appearances of bread and wine remain, a change which the Catholic Church most fittingly calls transubstantiation: let them be anathema.
[Can. 3] If anyone denies that in the venerable sacrament of the Eucharist the whole Christ is contained under each appearance, and under every part of each appearance when it is divided: let them be anathema.
[Can. 4] If anyone says that after the consecration is completed the body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ are not in the wonderful sacrament of the Eucharist, but only in its use, while it is being received, and not before or after; and that the true body of the Lord does not remain in the consecrated hosts or particles that are reserved or left over after communion: let them be anathema.
[Can. 5] If anyone says either that the chief fruit of the most holy Eucharist is the forgiveness of sins, or that no other effects come from it: let them be anathema.
[Can. 6] If anyone says that in the holy sacrament of the Eucharist Christ, the only-begotten Son of God, is not to be adored with the worship of latria, outward worship included; and so that he is not to be honoured with a special festive celebration, nor carried solemnly in processions according to the praiseworthy and universal rite and custom of holy Church, nor set publicly before the people to be adored; and that those who adore him are idolaters: let them be anathema.
[Can. 7] If anyone says that it is not lawful for the holy Eucharist to be reserved in a sacred place, but that it must be given out to those present immediately after the consecration; or that it is not lawful for it to be carried with honour to the sick: let them be anathema.
[Can. 8] If anyone says that Christ, given in the Eucharist, is eaten only spiritually, and not also sacramentally and really: let them be anathema.
[Can. 9] If anyone denies that each and every one of Christ's faithful, of both sexes, is bound, once they reach the age of discretion, to receive communion every year, at least at Easter, according to the precept of holy Mother Church: let them be anathema.
[Can. 10] If anyone says that it is not lawful for the celebrating priest to give communion to himself: let them be anathema.
[Can. 11] If anyone says that faith alone is sufficient preparation for receiving the sacrament of the most holy Eucharist: let them be anathema.
+ And so that so great a sacrament may not be received unworthily, and so to death and condemnation, this holy Council decrees and declares that those who are burdened by awareness of mortal sin, however contrite they may think themselves, must first make a sacramental confession, if a confessor is available. If anyone presumes to teach, preach or stubbornly assert the contrary, or even to defend it in public debate, they are by that very fact excommunicated.
