#! Council of Trent, Session 22 (17 September 1562), Doctrine on the Most Holy Sacrifice of the Mass — translated for Lumina from the Latin.

# Proem
[Proem] The holy, ecumenical and general Council of Trent, lawfully assembled in the Holy Spirit, with the same legates of the apostolic see presiding, wishes the ancient, complete and in every way perfect faith and teaching about the great mystery of the Eucharist to be kept in the holy Catholic Church, and to be preserved in its purity, with errors and heresies driven off. So, taught by the light of the Holy Spirit, it teaches and declares the following about the Eucharist as a true and unique sacrifice, and decrees that it be preached to the faithful.

# Chapter 1 · The institution of the most holy sacrifice of the Mass
[1] Under the former covenant, as the Apostle Paul testifies, there was no perfection, because the Levitical priesthood was weak (Hebrews 7:11, 18–19). So it was necessary, by the plan of God the Father of mercies, that another priest should arise "after the order of Melchizedek" (Genesis 14:18; Psalm 110:4; Hebrews 5:6, 10; 7:11, 17), our Lord Jesus Christ, who could make perfect and bring to completion all who were to be made holy (Hebrews 10:14).
+ He, then, our God and Lord, was to offer himself once to God the Father on the altar of the cross, by his death, so as to win there an eternal redemption (Hebrews 9:12). But because his priesthood was not to come to an end with his death (Hebrews 7:24, 27), at the Last Supper, "on the night when he was betrayed" (1 Corinthians 11:23), he wished to leave to his beloved Bride the Church a visible sacrifice, as human nature requires. By it the bloody sacrifice that was to be accomplished once for all on the cross would be made present, its memory would last until the end of the age (1 Corinthians 11:23–26), and its saving power would be applied for the forgiveness of the sins we commit every day.
+ So, declaring himself "a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek", he offered his body and blood to God the Father under the appearances of bread and wine. Under the signs of those same things he gave them to the apostles, whom he was then making priests of the New Covenant, to receive; and by these words he commanded them and their successors in the priesthood to offer them: "Do this in remembrance of me" (Luke 22:19; 1 Corinthians 11:24). This is how the Catholic Church has always understood and taught it.
+ For after he had celebrated the old Passover, which the assembly of the children of Israel sacrificed in memory of their going out from Egypt (Exodus 12:1–28), he instituted a new Passover: himself, to be offered by the Church through her priests under visible signs, in memory of his passing over from this world to the Father, when by the shedding of his blood he redeemed us, "rescued us from the power of darkness, and brought us into his kingdom" (Colossians 1:13).
+ And this is that clean offering which cannot be stained by any unworthiness or malice of those who offer it. It is the offering which the Lord foretold through Malachi would be offered pure in every place to his name, which would be great among the nations (Malachi 1:11). The Apostle Paul points to it clearly when he writes to the Corinthians that those who have been defiled by sharing in the table of demons cannot share in the table of the Lord (1 Corinthians 10:21), meaning by "table" in both cases the altar. Finally, it is the offering that was foreshadowed by the various kinds of sacrifice in the time of nature and of the Law (Genesis 4:4; 8:20; 12:8; 22; Exodus throughout), since it includes all the good things those sacrifices signified, as their completion and perfection.

# Chapter 2 · The sacrifice of the Mass is propitiatory, for the living and the dead
[2] In this divine sacrifice that is celebrated in the Mass, the same Christ is contained and offered in an unbloody manner who once offered himself in a bloody manner on the altar of the cross (Hebrews 9:14, 27–28). So the holy Council teaches that this sacrifice is truly propitiatory, and that through it, if we draw near to God with a sincere heart and right faith, with fear and reverence, contrite and repentant, "we receive mercy and find grace to help in time of need" (Hebrews 4:16). For the Lord, appeased by this offering, grants grace and the gift of repentance, and forgives crimes and sins, even great ones.
+ For the victim is one and the same: the same one now offers through the ministry of priests who then offered himself on the cross; only the manner of offering is different. The fruits of that bloody offering are received most abundantly through this unbloody one; so far is this from taking anything away from that. That is why, in keeping with the tradition of the apostles, it is rightly offered not only for the sins, punishments, satisfactions and other needs of the faithful who are living, but also for those who have died in Christ and are not yet fully purified.

# Chapter 3 · Masses in honour of the saints
[3] The Church has sometimes been accustomed to celebrate certain Masses in honour and memory of the saints. Yet she does not teach that the sacrifice is offered to them, but to God alone, who crowned them. So the priest does not say, "I offer this sacrifice to you, Peter," or "to you, Paul." Rather, giving thanks to God for their victories, he asks their help, so that those whose memory we celebrate on earth may be pleased to intercede for us in heaven.

# Chapter 4 · The Canon of the Mass
[4] Holy things should be handled in a holy way, and this sacrifice is the holiest of all. So, that it might be offered and received worthily and reverently, the Catholic Church many centuries ago established the sacred Canon. It is so free from all error that it contains nothing that does not breathe holiness and devotion in the highest degree and lift up to God the minds of those who offer it. For it is made up of the words of the Lord himself, of the traditions of the apostles, and of the devout decrees of holy popes.

# Chapter 5 · The ceremonies and rites of the Mass
[5] Human nature is such that it cannot easily be lifted up to meditate on divine things without outward helps. For this reason holy Mother Church has established certain rites, such as that some parts of the Mass are said in a low voice and others in a louder one. She has also brought in ceremonies, such as mystical blessings, lights, incense, vestments and many other things of this kind, from apostolic discipline and tradition. By these the majesty of so great a sacrifice is made plain, and the minds of the faithful are roused, through these visible signs of religion and devotion, to contemplate the most high realities hidden in this sacrifice.

# Chapter 6 · Masses at which only the priest receives communion
[6] The holy Council would indeed wish that at every Mass the faithful who are present should receive communion not only by spiritual desire but also by sacramental reception of the Eucharist, so that a richer fruit of this most holy sacrifice might come to them. But if this does not always happen, it does not on that account condemn as private and unlawful those Masses at which only the priest receives communion sacramentally. It approves them, and indeed commends them. For those Masses too must be considered truly communal: partly because in them the people receive communion spiritually, and partly because they are celebrated by a public minister of the Church, not for himself alone but for all the faithful who belong to the body of Christ.

# Chapter 7 · The water to be mixed with the wine offered in the chalice
[7] The holy Council next points out that the Church has commanded priests to mix water with the wine to be offered in the chalice. This is because it is believed that Christ the Lord did so, and also because water came out of his side together with blood (John 19:34), a mystery recalled by this mixing. And since in the Revelation of blessed John the waters are said to be the peoples (Revelation 17:1, 15), the union of the faithful people with Christ their head is shown in it.

# Chapter 8 · The Mass is not to be celebrated in the vernacular everywhere; its mysteries are to be explained to the people
[8] Although the Mass contains much instruction for the faithful, the Fathers did not think it advisable that it should be celebrated everywhere in the vernacular. So, while the ancient rite of each church, approved by the holy Roman Church, the mother and teacher of all churches, is kept everywhere, and so that the sheep of Christ may not go hungry, nor the little ones ask for bread with no one to break it for them (Lamentations 4:4), the holy Council commands pastors and all who have the care of souls to explain often during the celebration of Mass, either themselves or through others, something of what is read in the Mass; and among other things to explain some mystery of this most holy sacrifice, especially on Sundays and feast days.

# Chapter 9 · Introduction to the canons that follow
[9] In our time many errors are being spread against this ancient faith, which is founded on the holy Gospel, the traditions of the apostles and the teaching of the holy Fathers, and many things are being taught and argued by many people. So the holy Council, after many serious discussions of these matters, carefully held, has decided, with the unanimous agreement of all the Fathers, to condemn and remove from holy Church, by the canons below, whatever is opposed to this most pure faith and sacred teaching.

# Canons on the Sacrifice of the Mass
[Can. 1] If anyone says that in the Mass a true and proper sacrifice is not offered to God, or that "to be offered" means nothing else than that Christ is given us to eat: let them be anathema.
[Can. 2] If anyone says that by the words "Do this in remembrance of me" (Luke 22:19; 1 Corinthians 11:24) Christ did not make the apostles priests, or did not ordain that they and other priests should offer his body and blood: let them be anathema.
[Can. 3] If anyone says that the sacrifice of the Mass is only a sacrifice of praise and thanksgiving, or a bare commemoration of the sacrifice accomplished on the cross, and not propitiatory; or that it benefits only the one who receives; or that it must not be offered for the living and the dead, for sins, punishments, satisfactions and other needs: let them be anathema.
[Can. 4] If anyone says that the sacrifice of the Mass blasphemes the most holy sacrifice of Christ accomplished on the cross, or takes away from it: let them be anathema.
[Can. 5] If anyone says that it is a deception to celebrate Masses in honour of the saints and to obtain their intercession with God, as the Church intends: let them be anathema.
[Can. 6] If anyone says that the Canon of the Mass contains errors and should therefore be abolished: let them be anathema.
[Can. 7] If anyone says that the ceremonies, vestments and outward signs which the Catholic Church uses in the celebration of Mass are provocations to ungodliness rather than aids to devotion: let them be anathema.
[Can. 8] If anyone says that Masses at which only the priest receives communion sacramentally are unlawful and should therefore be abolished: let them be anathema.
[Can. 9] If anyone says that the rite of the Roman Church, by which part of the Canon and the words of consecration are said in a low voice, is to be condemned; or that the Mass must be celebrated only in the vernacular; or that water must not be mixed with the wine to be offered in the chalice because this is against Christ's institution: let them be anathema.
