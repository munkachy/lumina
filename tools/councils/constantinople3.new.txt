#! Third Council of Constantinople (Sixth Ecumenical Council), Session 18 (16 September 681), Definition of Faith — translated for Lumina from the Greek and Latin of the Acts.

# The Council
[1] The holy, great and ecumenical Council, assembled by the grace of God and by the decree of the most devout and faithful great Emperor Constantine, in this God-guarded imperial city of Constantinople, New Rome, in the domed hall of the imperial palace, has decreed as follows.
[2] The only-begotten Son and Word of God the Father, who became man like us in all things except sin, Christ our true God, declared plainly in the words of the Gospel: "I am the light of the world; whoever follows me will not walk in darkness, but will have the light of life" (John 8:12); and again, "Peace I leave with you; my peace I give to you" (John 14:27). Our most gentle Emperor, champion of the true faith and enemy of false teaching, was led reverently by this divinely spoken teaching on peace. He gathered this holy and ecumenical assembly and brought together the mind of the whole Church.

# The Councils and the Creed
[3] So this holy and ecumenical Council has driven out the ungodly error that had prevailed for some time until now. Following without turning aside the straight path of the holy and approved Fathers, it has given its devout agreement to the five holy and ecumenical councils: to the 318 holy Fathers who met at Nicaea against the raging Arius; to the next, the 150 God-inspired men at Constantinople, against Macedonius, who fought the Spirit, and the ungodly Apollinaris; to the first council at Ephesus, of 200 venerable men, against Nestorius; to the council at Chalcedon of 630 God-inspired Fathers, against Eutyches and Dioscorus; and, besides these, to the last, the fifth holy council, assembled in this city, against Theodore of Mopsuestia, Origen, Didymus and Evagrius, and against the writings of Theodoret attacking the Twelve Chapters of the renowned Cyril, and the letter said to have been written by Ibas to Mari the Persian. It renews in every point the ancient decrees of true religion and drives away the ungodly doctrines of irreligion. And this holy and ecumenical Council, inspired by God, has set its seal on the Creed set out by the 318 Fathers and confirmed again with reverence by the 150, which the other holy councils also received gladly and confirmed, for the removal of every heresy that destroys the soul.
[4] [Here the Council recites in full the Creed of the 318 holy Fathers of Nicaea, and the Creed of the 150 holy Fathers gathered at Constantinople: "We believe in one God…"]

# The new error
[5] This holy Council goes on to say: this devout and orthodox Creed of divine grace would have been enough for full knowledge and confirmation of the true faith. But the author of evil, who in the beginning used the serpent as his helper and so brought the poison of death on the human race, has not given up. In the same way, now, he has found suitable tools for his purpose: Theodore, who was bishop of Pharan; Sergius, Pyrrhus, Paul and Peter, who were archbishops of this imperial city; and also Honorius, who was pope of elder Rome; Cyrus, who was bishop of Alexandria; Macarius, who was lately bishop of Antioch; and Stephen his disciple. Through them he has been busy setting before the whole Church the stumbling block of one will and one operation in the two natures of Christ our true God, one of the Holy Trinity. In new words he has been sowing among the orthodox people a heresy like the mad and evil teaching of the ungodly Apollinaris, Severus and Themistius. He has been trying cunningly to destroy the completeness of the incarnation of our Lord Jesus Christ, our God, by blasphemously making out that his flesh, which has a rational soul, has no will and no operation of its own.
[6] So Christ our God has raised up our faithful Emperor, a new David, finding him a man after his own heart (Acts 13:22), who, as it is written, would "not give sleep to his eyes or slumber to his eyelids" (Psalm 132:4) until he had found, through this holy council gathered by God, a perfect statement of the true faith. For by the word of God, "where two or three are gathered in my name, there am I among them" (Matthew 18:20). This holy and ecumenical Council faithfully receives, and welcomes with uplifted hands, the letter sent to our most devout and faithful Emperor Constantine by the most holy and blessed Agatho, Pope of elder Rome, which rejects by name those who taught or preached one will and one operation in the incarnate life of our Lord Jesus Christ, our true God. In the same way it has adopted the other synodal letter, sent to his God-taught Serenity by the council of 125 God-loving bishops held under the same most holy Pope, as being in agreement with the holy Council of Chalcedon and with the Tome of the most holy and blessed Leo, Pope of the same elder Rome, sent to St Flavian, which that Council called the pillar of right faith. It agrees also with the synodal letters written by blessed Cyril against the ungodly Nestorius and to the bishops of the East.

# The Definition
[7] Following the five holy and ecumenical councils and the holy and approved Fathers, and defining with one voice, it confesses our Lord Jesus Christ, our true God, one of the holy, consubstantial and life-giving Trinity: perfect in divinity and perfect in humanity, truly God and truly man, made of a rational soul and a body; consubstantial with the Father in his divinity and consubstantial with us in his humanity, like us in all things except sin (Hebrews 4:15); begotten of the Father before the ages in his divinity, and in the last days, for us and for our salvation, born of the Holy Spirit and of the Virgin Mary, who is properly and truly the Mother of God, in his humanity. One and the same Christ, Son, Lord, only-begotten, made known in two natures without confusion, without change, without division, without separation. The difference of the natures is in no way taken away by the union; rather, the property of each nature is kept, and both come together in one person and one hypostasis. He is not split or divided into two persons, but is one and the same only-begotten Son, God the Word, the Lord Jesus Christ, as the prophets taught of old about him, as Jesus Christ himself taught us, and as the Creed of the holy Fathers handed on to us.
[8] In the same way, following the teaching of the holy Fathers, we proclaim that there are in him two natural willings, or wills, and two natural operations, without division, without change, without separation, without confusion. The two natural wills are not opposed to each other, God forbid, as the ungodly heretics have claimed. His human will follows, not resisting or struggling against it, but rather submitting to his divine and almighty will. For, as the most wise Athanasius says, the will of the flesh had to be moved, but to be subject to the divine will.
+ For just as his flesh is called, and is, the flesh of God the Word, so the natural will of his flesh is called, and is, God the Word's own will. As he himself says: "I have come down from heaven, not to do my own will, but the will of the Father who sent me" (John 6:38). He calls the will of the flesh his own will, because the flesh too became his own. For just as his all-holy and spotless flesh, with its soul, was not destroyed by being deified, but remained in its own limit and nature, so his human will, though deified, was not destroyed, but rather preserved. As Gregory the Theologian says: "His willing, understood as that of the Saviour, is not opposed to God, but is wholly deified."
[9] We give glory to two natural operations in the same Lord Jesus Christ, our true God, without division, without change, without separation, without confusion: that is, a divine operation and a human operation. As Leo, the herald of God, says most clearly: "Each form does what is proper to it, in communion with the other: the Word does what belongs to the Word, and the flesh carries out what belongs to the flesh."
[10] For we will not allow one natural operation of God and of the creature, so that we neither raise what is created into the divine being, nor bring down the excellence of the divine nature to the level proper to created things.
[11] For we recognize that the miracles and the sufferings belong to one and the same, but according to one or the other of the natures from which he is and in which he has his being, as the wonderful Cyril says. Keeping, then, on every side the "without confusion" and the "without division", we sum up the whole in a few words. We believe that our Lord Jesus Christ, our true God, is one of the Holy Trinity even after the incarnation. We say that his two natures shine out in his one hypostasis, in which he showed both the miracles and the sufferings through the whole of his life in the flesh, not in appearance but truly. The difference of the natures is recognized within that one hypostasis by the fact that each nature, in communion with the other, wills and works what is proper to it. In this way we give glory to two natural wills and operations, coming together fittingly in him for the salvation of the human race.

# Sanction
[12] Since we have set this out with all care and exactness, we decree that no one may put forward another faith, or write, compose, think or teach it otherwise. Those who dare to compose another faith, or to put forward, teach or hand on another creed to those who wish to turn to the knowledge of the truth from paganism or Judaism or any heresy whatever, or to bring in new words or invented terms to overturn what has now been defined: if they are bishops or clergy, they are to be deposed, bishops from the episcopate and clergy from the clergy; if they are monks or lay people, they are to be anathema.
