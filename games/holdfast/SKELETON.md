# HOLDFAST — skeleton

**Nothing is written yet.** This is the document to argue with. Every want below
is a claim I am making about why somebody does something, and any one of them
you reject is cheap to change now and expensive to change in eleven thousand
words of prose.

---

## The one-sentence premise

Forty-one people are living in a closed parish school with nine days of quiet
left, and you are the one deciding what gets built with the time.

**Super-objective (yours, all game):** *I want these people to still be here at
the end of the winter.* Every scene want below is a local version of that.

**What makes it hard:** you cannot build everything, you do not know what is
coming, and the work you spend finding out what is coming is work you did not
spend on the wall.

---

## The place

**St. Basil's**, a Catholic grade school closed in 2009, and the church attached
to it by a covered walk. Brick. Chain-link on three sides and a loading dock on
the fourth. A gym, a kitchen with two gas ranges and no gas, a boiler room, a
nurse's office, a playground with four inches of topsoil under the asphalt, a
dry well the parish capped in 1988, and a bell tower with a view down the
highway to the county line.

Forty-one people. Nine days before the weather turns and the roads move.

## The cast — four people, one line each

| | Want, all game | Voice |
|---|---|---|
| **You** | Everyone alive in spring | Second person; a foreman's eye — you see loads, spans, and how long a job takes |
| **Fr. Peter Nowak**, 52 | To keep them a parish and not a garrison | Asks rather than tells. Will do any job assigned and will keep asking the question while he does it |
| **Dell Marchetti**, 61, retired ironworker | To be useful before his hands go | Trade talk. Measures out loud. Never says a feeling; describes a job instead |
| **Ruthie Okonkwo**, 29, was a vet tech | To not be the one who decides who is sick enough | Precise, clipped, asks for numbers. The only one who says the frightening thing at the table |

## The ten projects — the only state this game keeps

Ten flags. Each one is a thing you could photograph. **Twelve work-days to
spend, and the ten projects cost thirty-one.**

| Project | Cost | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `wall` | 4 | The chain-link doubled with lockers, rebar and poured footings |
| `gate` | 2 | A vehicle gate one person can close in nine seconds |
| `tower` | 2 | A platform and a clear sightline four miles down the highway |
| `well` | 4 | The 1988 cap off, a hand pump, and water that is not from the roof |
| `garden` | 3 | The asphalt up, the beds in, and something planted before the freeze |
| `clinic` | 3 | The nurse's office cleared, instruments boiled, two people trained |
| `heat` | 4 | The boiler on wood, and ducting to two rooms instead of nine |
| `quiet` | 2 | Blackout, muffled doors, and a discipline everybody has agreed to |
| `radio` | 3 | The school PA amplifier on a mast — you can hear, and be heard |
| `chapel` | 4 | The church opened, the roof patched, the lamp lit and kept lit |

**Scouting costs work too.** Each of the four scouting trips costs 1 day and
tells you which crisis is likeliest. That is the central decision of the game:
*eleven days of building and no idea, or eight days of building and a good guess.*

---

## The five crises — one arrives, weighted by what you learned

Each is telegraphed by something visible during the build. Each tests **two**
projects, and no project is in every pair.

| Crisis | Sign you can find | Tests | Everything else gives partial credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The column** — thousands moving down the highway | Crows going the same way three mornings running | `wall` + `quiet` | `tower` buys hours; `chapel` holds people still |
| **The freeze** — nine days at minus twenty | The maples turned three weeks early | `heat` + `well` | `garden` feeds; `clinic` keeps toes |
| **The living** — eleven armed people who want in | A truck that turns around at the county line twice | `gate` + `tower` | `radio` lets you talk first; `wall` matters less than you think |
| **The sickness** — dysentery in a building with one toilet | The stream below the loading dock is wrong | `clinic` + `well` | `quiet` is useless here and that is the point |
| **The fire** — the school's east wing, at night | The boiler flue is cracked and nobody has looked | `heat` + `garden` (the firebreak) | `gate` gets people out; `tower` sees it early |

## The four choices that no wall can answer

These sit **outside** the build and cannot be solved by it. This is where the
story is.

1. **Day two — nine people at the gate.** Nine more pairs of hands, nine more
   mouths, and a winter that was already tight. *Fr. Nowak wants them in. Ruthie
   wants a number.*
2. **Day six — Dell's hands.** He is failing and hiding it and the wall crew is
   his. *He wants to finish the job. You want the footings to hold.*
3. **The crisis — somebody is outside when the gate has to shut.** Nine seconds
   is the design spec you either built or did not.
4. **After — one person is sick and you have four doses.** Ruthie will do it if
   you tell her to, and she is asking you not to make her decide.

## The build menu — how spending works

At scenes 3 and 7 you get the same list with the costs and the days remaining,
and you take projects until the days run out. That is one node with a
conditional list, not thirty branches. Each purchase sets a flag and subtracts.
Scenes 5 and any other scouting trip take from the same pot.

**Nothing is hidden.** The costs, the days left, and what each project is
believed to be good for are all on the page. The unknown is which crisis comes,
and that is what scouting is for.

## What I would like you to reject

Places where I have guessed and would rather be told:

1. **Twelve days against thirty-one days of work.** That is a 39% budget. It
   might want to be 45% — tight enough to hurt, loose enough that two good
   guesses feel like a plan.
2. **Five crises may be one too many** for 11,000 words. Four gives every crisis
   more room.
3. **Fr. Nowak's want.** *To keep them a parish and not a garrison* is the most
   interesting thing about him and also the thing most likely to be wrong. He
   could equally want to leave, or to have died in the first week and be ashamed
   of not having.
4. **Whether the priest should be right.** The chapel project costs four days —
   as much as the wall. If it never pays off, it is a tax on piety. If it always
   pays off, it is a sermon. My instinct is that it pays off in exactly two of
   the five crises and everyone can see which.
