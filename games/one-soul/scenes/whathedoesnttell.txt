*comment ---- 8.1 : the scale changes ----

In the autumn of 1996 a modem goes into the apartment on Semple Street.

It is for the four of them and it costs nineteen dollars a month and it makes a noise like a small animal being posted somewhere.

*page_break

Here is how this scene is going to be told, and it is not squeamishness, it is the shape of the thing:

You cannot see him.

Not for any of it. Not once. The dark comes down the way it came down in 1989 and it comes down more often now, and what you get is what is left over — the hours, and the light under a door, and what a room looks like at four in the morning, and a young man who used to be on a roof by six and is now getting up at eleven.

*set_ward sight "dark"

*page_break

You know the shape of it from outside, the way you know weather from inside a house.

It is not the same as 1989. In 1989 it took effort — it took a magazine somebody's brother had, it took going somewhere, it took being a person who had done something. The effort was part of the machinery: he had to decide, each time, to be that.

There is no effort now. That is the entire difference and it is not a small one.

And she notices the pause and not the reason for it, and she files it, the way people do, in the place where you keep things that do not fit and cannot be asked about.

*comment ---- the road that is not a magic fix ----

Here is what telling her does and does not do.

It does not fix him. He does not stop. He stops for eleven weeks and then he does not stop, and she knows about it now, which is its own kind of grinding for both of them.

It does not make her wise. She is twenty-one and she handles it imperfectly, sometimes badly, and there are three or four things she says in 1996 that she would give a year of her life to take back.

*page_break

What it does is this.

He remains, to himself, one person.

There is no second man in the apartment on Semple Street. There is no room in him with a different Adam in it. Everything he does is done by somebody who is known, by at least one other human being, to be doing it — and a thing that is known is a thing that can still be talked about, and a thing that can be talked about is not yet a room with a door on it.

That is all it buys. It is a small thing and it costs an enormous amount and the game is not going to pretend otherwise.

*comment ---- 8.3 : what concealment actually does ----

He keeps it, and here is what you expect: distance.

That is what you expect because that is what everybody expects. He will pull back. There will be less of him in the room. He will be somewhere else while he is standing there.

*page_break

That is not what happens.

What happens is a *hierarchy.*

*page_break

He knows something she does not.

He has looked at what people actually are, in the middle of the night, at close range, and she has not. He is honest with himself about the world; she lives in something comfortable that he is protecting her from. He is *free*, in a way that is slightly sad and slightly heroic, and she is naive, in a way that is sweet and requires managing.

He would be appalled if you said any of this to him. He does not think it in words. Not once, not ever.

*page_break

And every week he does not tell her, that gap gets a little wider, and something moves in to fill it.

*page_break

*comment ---- Loretta's misjudgment, inverted ----

"That's not the worst part, though," you say. "Whatever else — that's not the *worst* part. He's twenty-one. Half the men I ever knew were doing something like that at twenty-one and most of them turned out fine."

Your one does not say anything.

"What."

"You have the two of them the wrong way round."

*page_break

"I'm sorry?"

"You have spent seven years appalled at what he does at four in the morning," it says, "and comfortable about what he does at the kitchen table. And I have spent seven years unable to tell you how much of the first one he is answerable for."

"And the second?"

"He is answerable for the second. All of it. Every week. He is twenty-one years old and he is entirely awake and he knows precisely what he is choosing and he chooses it again on Tuesday."

*page_break

"It's just *not telling her something.*"

"It is deciding, once a week for two years, that a person he loves is a lesser kind of creature than he is," your one says. "There is a word for that and it is not one of the words you were watching for. You were watching for the one they warn girls about in 1965."

*comment ---- 8.4 : the kitchen table. 1997 ----

Semple Street, a Thursday in October 1997, about nine at night. There is a kitchen table with a burn on it from before either of them lived there.

She says: "What's wrong?"

He says: "Nothing's wrong."

*page_break

And he is telling the truth.

That is the thing to sit with. Whatever he has or has not told her, at this table, at nine o'clock, when he is asked directly, he is not conscious of concealing anything. Nothing feels wrong to him. He has a girlfriend he has known since he was nine and an apartment and a degree coming and there is genuinely nothing he could name.

She can see there is. She has been able to see it since February of 1996.

And she is twenty-two and she is a coward about exactly one thing in her whole life, which is conflict, and she has asked once, and he has said nothing's wrong, and she lets it go.

*page_break

She will think about that for twenty years.

*page_break

There are two angels in this kitchen. They have been within eleven feet of each other for twelve years. Neither of them can say a word to either of these people and both of them can see the whole thing, and the table is four feet across.

*page_break
