*comment ---- 11.1 : THE DRAWER. March 2016, age forty-one ----

Stan Kowalczyk dies on the eleventh of March, 2016, at seventy-nine, of the thing that gets most men who worked on that river.

It takes nine days in a hospital and he does not say very much, which surprises nobody.

*page_break

Adam clears the house in April. His mother has gone to his aunt's and cannot be in the building yet.

He does it alone over two weekends, badly, in the way people do — three hours on a shelf of nothing and then a whole room in forty minutes.

And in the second drawer of a bureau, under a box of screws, there is a spiral notebook that was bought at a drugstore on Liberty Avenue for fifty-nine cents.

*page_break

He opens it standing up.

*page_break

There is one word at the top of the first page, in pencil, and the word is his own name.

And under it there is a date — 21.11.93 — and then another date, and another, in a column, in a hand that gets worse as it goes down the page and then worse again on the next page and worse again for two hundred and eighty-three pages, and the last date is the fourth of March, 2016, seven days before he died, in handwriting you can barely read.

Twenty-three years. Every single day. And not one word of comment anywhere in it. Just the name at the top and the dates going down.

In the same second that you finally understand what it was.

*page_break

Adam Kowalczyk sits down on the floor of his father's bedroom with a notebook in his hands and does not get up for about twenty minutes.

*comment ---- 11.2 : EIGHTEEN MONTHS OF CHECKING. 2017–18 ----

It does not begin as anything.

In the autumn of 2016 he starts reading at night, mostly because he is not sleeping, and mostly things that are not about this at all. In February of 2017 he reads something that annoys him, and in looking up why it annoys him he finds something that annoys him more.

He is not searching. He is a forty-two-year-old man who is *checking* — the way you check a bill you are fairly sure is right.

*page_break

It is not there.

*page_break

*comment ---- 11.3 : NINETY SECONDS. 2018, age forty-three ----

He does not go home.

He drives to a church in Swissvale that he has no connection to whatsoever, where there is no line at all, and sits in the back for twenty minutes, and then goes in.

*page_break

*comment ---- 11.5 : SIX YEARS. 2018–2024 ----

He is not a different man.

That is the thing this has to be honest about. He is the same man, facing a different direction, badly, most days.

He is late to Mass constantly. He goes to the eleven because he cannot get up for the nine. He does not join anything. He would not be able to give you an account of what he believes if you put a gun to his head; he could give you an account of what he has stopped arguing with.

*page_break

He is kinder than he was. He could not tell you why and does not notice.

He goes to see his mother twice a week instead of twice a month, and Ewa Kowalczyk, who is seventy-one and sharp as a tack, notices immediately and says nothing whatsoever about it for six years.
