*comment ---- 10.2 : Wilkinsburg. 2001, age twenty-six ----

A man at work called Curtis Hale invites him to a service in Wilkinsburg on a Sunday in March 2001, in the way people do — three times, casually, over five months, with no pressure and no follow-up when he says no.

The fourth time Adam says yes because he has nothing on.

*page_break

The building used to be a bank. There are two hundred people in it and they are pleased to see each other in a way that is not performed. Somebody's grandmother makes him sit down and puts a bulletin in his hand. The music goes on considerably longer than he expects and about eight minutes into it he stops looking at his watch.

Nobody has an angle. That is the thing he cannot get past afterwards. He came in braced for one and there is not one.

*page_break

At the end they pray for people by name, out loud, and Curtis puts a hand on his shoulder without asking and says his name into a room of two hundred strangers, and about forty of them say it back.

*comment ---- 10.4 : 2007 ----

In 2007 a crowd shows up.

There are books in the airport shops and a documentary and three people at work who have opinions now, and for about two years Adam Kowalczyk feels genuinely, physically lighter.

*page_break

And here is what that is about, and it is not argument.

He has believed all of this since he was eighteen. Nothing he encounters in 2007 tells him one thing he had not already decided on a bus in February of 1993.

What is new is that there are *other people*, in numbers, saying it out loud, in public, with charts. He has been carrying it privately for fourteen years and now he is not carrying it, and the relief of that is enormous and has nothing whatever to do with whether any of it is true.

*page_break

"He was lonely," you say.

"He was lonely," your angel agrees.

"That's all it was. Fourteen years of it and it was just —"

"It was mostly that. There is an argument in there too and it is not a bad one. But you have put your finger on the load-bearing part."

*comment ---- 10.5 : nothing to do ----

And then there are the years where there is nothing.

*page_break

He gets up, he goes to a job he does not mind, he comes home, he watches something, he goes to bed. He is not in danger. He is not in grace either, particularly. He is thirty-five years old and nothing is happening to him at all.

You find this much harder than 1989.
