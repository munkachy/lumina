You are seventy-one years old and you have been awake since four.

The room has a window in it, which the brochure made a great deal of. What the window looks at is the third level of a parking structure. If you angle yourself and it is early enough, you can see a piece of sky over the top of it, about the size of a bath towel.

It is early enough. The sky is doing nothing.

Your sister went home Sunday. The physical therapist comes at ten and is twenty-four years old and says *we* when she means *you*. There is a cup of apple juice on the tray that has been there since last night, and somebody will take it away eventually, and until then it is yours.

They took the second leg on Thursday.

*page_break

On the windowsill there are fourteen porcelain angels.

You asked for them. Your niece brought them in two shoeboxes with dish towels between the layers, and she set them up in a row while you watched, and she was so pleased about it that you said thank you twice.

Every one of them is a woman in a nightgown.

Six have harps. Two have their eyes closed in a way you did not use to mind. One is holding a lamb, and you have owned it for thirty-one years and you have never once worked out what the lamb is doing there. One is a raffle prize from the sodality in 1994 and is not, when you look at it honestly, very well made.

And one is from Walter, the Christmas before he died, and it says GUARDIAN ANGEL WATCHING OVER ME on the base in gold, and he was not a man who bought that kind of thing, and he must have stood in a shop and picked it up and thought: yes. That one.

You have prayed to yours every day of your adult life. Out loud, most days. You made up a name for it in 1979 because nobody ever gave you one and it seemed rude to keep saying *hey*.

*page_break

You put your hand out.

It is not a rampage. It is one motion with your forearm, and it takes about a second and a half, and then it is very loud and then it is very quiet and you are still sitting there with your arm out.

Nobody comes. Somewhere down the hall a machine is going off and it has been going off for ten minutes and nobody is coming for that either.

You say, to the ceiling, in a completely ordinary voice:

I have talked to you every day since 1979.

I made up a name for you because nobody ever gave me one.

I have got nothing back in forty-five years that I could point at and say, that. That was you.

And they took the other leg on Thursday.

That is the whole prayer. It is not blasphemy. There is a whole book of the Bible where a man says considerably worse and God's complaint at the end of it is not about the tone.

*comment ---- 0.3 : something is in the room ----

There is something in the room.

You know before you look, the way you know when a person has come up behind you in a kitchen. And when you look, the first thing you notice is not the size of it and not the light coming off it.

The first thing you notice is that it is not looking at you with any expression at all.

Not a cold expression. Not a kind one. Expression is a thing that faces do, and there is no face. There is nothing in the room that is arranged for you to read.

It does not have a harp. It does not have a nightgown. It is not a woman. It is not a man either, and the more you look at it the more you understand that neither of those words was ever going to be any use here, and that you have spent forty-five years talking to something you had the wrong shape for entirely.

You are not afraid, which surprises you.

You are furious, which does not.
