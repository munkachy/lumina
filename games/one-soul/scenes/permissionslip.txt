*comment ---- 9.1 : forty seconds in a bar ----

There is a bar on Forbes with a shuffleboard table nobody uses and it is the second week of November, 1997.

Adam is there with three other people, one of whom he half knows, and one of whom is a graduate student in something, twenty-six, teaching two sections of an introductory course and holding forth about it, three drinks in.

He is not a bad man. He is not even wrong about most of what he says. He is twenty-six and he has just been given an idea to hold and he is holding it the way you hold a new tool, turning it over, showing it to people.

*page_break

And at some point in the evening he says a sentence.

It is about Christian morality. It is roughly that the whole apparatus was put together by people who could not do anything and needed a reason why not doing it was better — that what gets called virtue is very often just an inability with a flag stuck in it.

He says it as an aside. He is not making an argument; he is repeating one, and he is repeating it slightly wrong, and he will not think about it again after Thursday.

*page_break

Adam Kowalczyk is twenty-two years old and is not, and never becomes, an intellectual.

He does not read the book. He never reads the book. He could not tell you the name of the man who wrote it and by 2003 he could not tell you the name of the graduate student either.

*page_break

What he takes home is the *sentence.*

And the sentence fits — not into an argument, because he does not have one, but into a shape that has been sitting empty in him since about April of 1996, waiting for exactly this size of thing.

*page_break

*comment ---- what she can see, and what it costs to see it ----

"He didn't even —" you say. "He wasn't *convinced* of anything. He was standing next to a man with a beer."

"No."

"That's it? That's the whole conversion? A *pub*?"

"He was not converted," your angel says. "Nothing about him changed on the ninth of November. He has believed the substance of that sentence since he was twenty-one and could not say it out loud, because when you say it out loud without a philosopher behind it, it sounds like what it is."

*page_break

"Which is what?"

"*I am better than the people who do not do what I do.*"

*page_break

You think about that for a while, standing in a bar on Forbes Avenue in 1997.

"That's it," you say. "That's the whole of the thing you told me about at the kitchen table."

"Yes."

"He got the contempt first and the reason afterwards."

"Two years afterwards," your angel says. "Nearly to the week."

*comment ---- 9.2 : what Legion does with it ----

And here is the part that takes you a while to notice.

They do almost nothing.

*page_break

They did not put the graduate student in the bar. They did not write the book, or the television programme, or the thing the coworker said in 2003. They did not construct one sentence of the argument and they could not have; they are not clever enough and they never were.

What they have done, since 1975, is one thing, patiently, over and over, in the dark, at four in the morning, and they have let a young man build every single other part of it himself.

*page_break

"That's not fair," you say. "That's — he's *building his own*."

"Yes."

"They're barely *doing* anything."

"They have never had to do very much," your angel says. "That is the thing about them that nobody believes until they have watched it for twenty years."

*page_break

*comment ---- 9.3 : the breakup. 1998, age twenty-three ----

He ends it in June.

He does it cleanly. He does it in a car outside her mother's house on a Sunday afternoon, and he is not cruel, and he does not lie, and he does not drag it out, and he says several things that are true.

He says that he is not the person she thinks. He says that she deserves somebody who is actually there. He says that stringing her along would be worse and that he is not going to do that to her.

*page_break

And for the rest of his life — for the next twenty-six years, in three apartments and two other relationships — this is going to be the thing Adam Kowalczyk tells himself when he needs to feel like a decent man.

*I told her the truth. It cost me everything and I told her the truth.*

*page_break

Renee Sobek is twenty-three years old and has known him since she was nine and she sits in a car outside her mother's house and cannot work out what she is being told or why it is happening today.

*page_break

*comment ---- and the machine does not make a sound ----

And now look at the panel.

*comment ---- 9.4 : her angel goes ----
