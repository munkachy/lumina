Nadire has a rope to the sky and it is broken nine hundred kilometres up.

You can see it on the approach: a thread of dark against the daylight side, forty thousand kilometres of it, anchored on a mountain at the equator and running out to a counterweight that was a captured rock before it was an anchor. It is the only reason there are eleven thousand people here. Nadire is a hard world with thin soil and a great deal of ore, and everything it eats comes down that ribbon and everything it sells goes up it.

The climber track failed at the nine-hundred-kilometre mark four months ago. The ribbon is intact. The track is not. Nothing has gone up or down since.

They have eleven months of food.

*comment ---------------- The offer ----------------

Warden **Sela Ruys** chairs the council of Nadire. She is about forty-five, she was a track engineer before she was a warden, and she has the particular exhaustion of a person who understands the problem completely and cannot solve it.

She sends you the file on the first day, unasked, complete, including the parts that do not help her.

The Vaunt's offer is four pages and it is not a trick. You read it three times looking for the trick.

**The Vaunt of Kes-Arahel will repair the climber track at the nine-hundred-kilometre station within one hundred and forty days, at its own cost, using its own yards, to a standard exceeding the original.**

**In consideration: Nadire accedes to the Vaunt as an associated world. Vaunt law in matters of trade and transit. Vaunt tax at the associated rate, being eleven per cent. Vaunt vessels to have the freedom of the harbour. Local government in all other matters, guaranteed, and reviewable at Nadire's request in twenty years.**

It is honest. That is the thing that keeps you awake: every number in it is true and every cost is stated on the page, including the eleven per cent, including the harbour.

On the sixth day the council of Nadire sits in open session, in a hall at the foot of a mountain with a rope going up out of it, and hears both governments.

There are eleven councillors and about nine hundred people in the public gallery and it is being carried live to every settlement on the world.

Sar Vehn Oleyo speaks for forty minutes. He does not persuade. He explains: the geometry of the splice, the hundred and forty days, the eleven per cent and what it buys and what it costs, the freedom of the harbour and exactly what a Vaunt vessel may and may not do in it, the review at twenty years and the fact that a review is not a right of exit and he is not going to pretend it is.

He is asked nine questions and he answers all nine, including the two that hurt him.

Then Warden Ruys says: "Commander. The Concord."

*page_break

You have nothing.

That is the actual position. The Concord cannot repair the track in four hundred days, cannot vote the money inside a year, cannot put a heavy yard vessel in the Cold Reach, and cannot offer association because the Concord does not do association; it does membership, and membership requires a vote of eleven worlds and takes, on the current form, nine years.

You are a commander of a twenty-six-year-old survey ship with orders to observe and report.

"An act is measured by what it produces. That is the whole of it and it sounds like nothing.

"Here is what it means. It means that on Kes-Arahel there is no such thing as clean hands. There is no rule you can obey that makes you not responsible for what happens. If a world starves and I had a ship and a cargo and a regulation, the regulation is not a defence; the starving is on my file, in my hand, for ever."

"Do you understand what that costs? Every one of my officers carries every consequence of every restraint. There is no *I was ordered*, no *the rule required it*, no *it was not my decision*. It is exhausting and it is why we are careful and it is why, when we come to a world with a broken rope, we come with a survey and a costing and a hundred and forty days, because saying *we cannot* is a thing that goes on my record as an act."

"And your Standing Order Four says: hold still, and what follows is not yours.

"Commander, I have read that sentence a hundred times and I have never once been able to decide whether it is the most cowardly thing a government has ever written down, or the only honest one."

On the tenth night the ribbon catches the sun after the mountain has gone dark, so that for about four minutes there is a thread of light going up out of a black world into a black sky, forty thousand kilometres of it, broken nine hundred kilometres up where nobody can see.

Eleven thousand people watch it. They do this every night. It is not a ceremony; it is just that it happens and everybody stops.
