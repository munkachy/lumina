Tessine Station is a wheel with eleven spokes and nine thousand people in it, most of whom are leaving.

It sits at the junction of four trade lanes and it exists to move things through: cargo, ships, crews, whole families under Concord resettlement warrants. Nobody is from Tessine. Everybody on it is between somewhere and somewhere else, except for about a thousand people who arrived fourteen months ago in the holds of a survey ship and have not moved since.

The approach is bad. Four lanes, a debris shell from sixty years of traffic, and a station that has been extended twice by people who were not talking to each other. Concord regulation requires a licensed harbour pilot aboard for the final eleven kilometres.

"Pilot boat's away and closing," ${xoname} says. "Two minutes."

Nadire: independent, eleven thousand people, a hard world with a good harbour and a failing orbital elevator that they cannot repair and cannot live without.

The Vaunt have offered to repair it.

That is the whole thing. No fleet, no ultimatum, no war. An offer, from people who are polite and exact and do not lie, to fix the one thing that is killing a world — and the price is association, which means Vaunt law, Vaunt tax, Vaunt ships in the harbour, forever.

Nadire is nine days away and it has asked the Concord for a consultation and the Concord has sent one ship with orders to watch.

You need a pilot to get off Tessine.

Ivo Halloran comes up through the airlock at 0400 in a jacket with the insignia taken off, takes the pilot's position at the back of the bridge of the ship he had for nine years, and cons her out through four lanes and a sixty-year debris shell without a wasted word.

At the eleventh kilometre he hands her back. And then, instead of picking up his case, he says:

"Captain. May I say a thing that is not a pilot's business?"
