# PERPETUA — canon ledger

Written before the prose. Re-read at the head of every scene. Nothing goes on
the page that contradicts this file; when the prose needs a new fact, it lands
here first.

---

## The world

**The Concord.** Eleven worlds bound by the Concord of Ilium, signed 143 years
ago at the end of the Fracture — a forty-year war among those same eleven. The
war did not end because anyone won. It ended because the survivors agreed to be
bound by something none of them controlled. The Concord therefore has almost no
power over its members by design. It cannot tax a world, cannot conscript from
one, cannot overrule one. It can only send ships.

**The Survey.** The Concord's one common institution: 214 ships that map,
contact, mediate, carry mail, and pull people out of burning atmospheres.
Officers swear to the Concord and not to their homeworld, which on some worlds
is still considered a betrayal. The Survey is not a navy. Its ships carry
weapons and everyone knows it.

**Standing Order Four — the Reticence.** The Survey's founding restraint, and
the source of most of this book's arguments. Its full text is two sentences:

> *No officer of the Survey shall move the history of a world that has not
> asked. Where a world has asked, the officer shall give the least that
> answers.*

Both clauses are traps. Who counts as asking — a government, a crowd, a dying
man, a child? And "the least that answers" is a judgment made in the dark,
every time, by one person, who is then judged for it.

**The Vaunt of Kes-Arahel.** Nine worlds and a better fleet, coreward. Governed
by the Weighing: an act is measured by what it produces, and by nothing else.
The Vaunt regard the Concord as a beautiful machine for avoiding
responsibility, and the Reticence as a way of letting people die politely.
Vaunt officers are courteous, exact, and do not lie — not from virtue but
because a lie is inefficient and they keep records. **They are never written as
villains. They are written as people who are sometimes right.**

**The Cold Reach.** Forty light years of thin, badly charted space on the
Concord's spinward edge, where the *Perpetua* is posted. Three member colonies,
eleven independent settlements, no Survey ship within nine days of any of them.

## The ship

**CS *Perpetua***, Meridian-class survey vessel, 26 years old, 112 crew.
Not a flagship. Two decks of laboratory, one of cargo, a hangar with two
shuttles (*Tern* and *Kittiwake*), a sickbay of nine beds, a chapel compartment
that used to be a scanner bay.

Survey ships are named for people who kept faith under duress. The plaque in
the corridor outside the bridge reads:

> **PERPETUA** — *She would not be moved, and she was not consoled, and she did
> not stop.*

Nobody aboard remembers who she was. The chaplain, if the player ships one,
knows. (Payoff: Chapter 9 or 12.)

**Physical handles, used consistently:** the deck plating in the aft corridor
rings differently than the rest of the ship. The number two coolant loop
whistles at exactly the frequency of a kettle. The bridge is small enough that
everyone hears everything.

## Captain Ivo Halloran

Master of *Perpetua* for nine years. Fourteen months before the game opens, at
the Ceyla system, he was ordered to hold position and observe. He evacuated the
colony on Ceyla III instead — nine hundred and six people — and in doing so put
his ship where the Vaunt could see it, which told the Vaunt that the Concord had
no fleet in the Reach.

The Vaunt took the Ceyla system eleven weeks later. Forty-one thousand people on
Ceyla II are now Vaunt subjects. They are not being mistreated. That is the
worst part.

The Survey's inquiry closed without a public finding. Halloran was retired at
his own request. **He would do it again, and he knows exactly what it cost.**

He is alive, on Tessine Station, working as a harbour pilot. He is not bitter.
The crew of the *Perpetua* were never told what happened, and about half of them
love him.

**Sar Vehn Oleyo took the Ceyla system.** Oleyo considers Halloran the most
honest officer in the Concord and says so, which is unbearable.

## The cast

Named crew are chosen by the player from three or four candidates per post. Each
candidate has one competence, one liability, and one handle that recurs.

### First Officer (`xo`)
- **Elu Marand**, 51. Passed over for command three times; wanted this ship.
  Correct, cold, and will tell you that you are wrong in front of the bridge
  crew. *Handle:* keeps a written log of your orders, by hand, in a paper book.
- **Ilya Ferreo**, 34. The Survey's favourite; a Concord delegate's son. Makes
  everything easier and nothing safer. *Handle:* knows everyone's first name
  within a day, and their mother's.
- **Bel Tanaka-Osei**, 43. Twelve years hauling cargo before the Survey took
  her. Reads people; does not read regulations. *Handle:* eats standing up,
  always, and offers you half of it.

### Chief Engineer (`eng`)
- **Wen Osimo**, 63. Nineteen years aboard *Perpetua*, longer than anyone alive.
  Loves the ship more than the mission and does not pretend otherwise.
  *Handle:* calls the ship "she" and the crew "the passengers."
- **Priya Kallas**, 29. Will get you a miracle and then explain, at length, why
  you should not have needed one. *Handle:* talks faster when frightened.
- **Anselm Dorr**, 44. A Vaunt defector, eight years in the Concord. The best
  engineer available to you, and every conversation with the Vaunt becomes
  harder because he is aboard. *Handle:* apologises before he disagrees.

### Ship's Surgeon (`med`)
- **Hadiya Nour**, 58. Field surgeon through the Ilium famine. Has seen worse
  than you and will say so. *Handle:* asks a patient's name twice, once at the
  start and once at the end.
- **Kip Sorrel**, 31. Warm, open, everyone's confessor, and carries every death
  personally. *Handle:* remembers birthdays; the whole ship knows he does.
- **Teodor Vasi**, 49. A researcher who took the post for the specimens. Sees a
  plague as a chance. Chilling, and useful. *Handle:* narrates his own work
  aloud, for the record.

### Security and Tactical (`sec`)
- **Rennick Oyelaran**, 40. Ex-Concord Marine. Believes the Survey is a navy
  that lies to itself about what it is. *Handle:* stands when you enter, every
  time, and will not stop when asked.
- **Sena Whitlow**, 37. A civil investigator, not a soldier. Thinks in evidence
  and will not be hurried. *Handle:* writes nothing down until you finish.
- **Mott**, age unclear. A Karsk: two and a half metres, grey, slow-spoken, and
  bound by a vow of her people not to end a life. Either the finest thing aboard
  or a hole in the hull, depending on the week. *Handle:* names things — the
  corridors, the shuttles, the storms.

### Chaplain (`chap`) — the berth may be left empty
- **Fr. Tobias Wren**, 47. Says Mass at 0600 in the old scanner bay. Will not
  tell you what to do. Will ask you the question you were avoiding. *Handle:*
  answers a question with a longer silence than is comfortable.
- **Sister Adaeze Onu**, 55. Of a nursing order; not a priest; doubles as a
  medic. Practical, tireless, unsentimental about souls and bodies alike.
  *Handle:* works while she talks, always, and never sits down first.
- **Loman Serat**, 38. Of the Survey's psychological corps. Competent, modern,
  and the ship sleeps better with him aboard. Nobody gets asked anything hard.
  *Handle:* rephrases what you said back to you, slightly improved.
- **Nobody.** The berth stays empty. The compartment stays a scanner bay.

### Not chosen by the player
- **Sar Vehn Oleyo**, master of the Vaunt cruiser *Ashaya*. Uses your rank
  scrupulously and never your name. Recurring. *Handle:* thanks you for things
  you did not intend to give.
- **Assessor Ruth Ivanescu**, Survey Board of Inquiry. Chapter 8.
- **Toma Beye**, *Perpetua*'s helmsman, 24, aboard four years, adored Halloran.
- **Ceron Adjo**, Concord Delegate for the Reach. Sends the orders you resent.
